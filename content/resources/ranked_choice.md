## What is Ranked Choice Voting?

Ranked Choice Voting (RCV) lets you rank candidates in order of preference — 1st, 2nd, 3rd,
and so on — rather than voting for just one.

## How does it work?

1. **You rank candidates.** On your ballot, you put a "1" next to your top choice, a "2" next
   to your second choice, and so on. You can rank as many or as few candidates as you like.

2. **First choices are counted.** If any candidate gets more than 50% of first-choice votes,
   they win outright.

3. **If no one reaches 50%**, the candidate with the fewest first-choice votes is eliminated.
   Anyone who ranked that eliminated candidate first has their vote transferred to their second
   choice.

4. **This repeats** until one candidate has more than 50% of the active votes.

## DC's Initiative 83

DC voters approved ranked choice voting for primary elections in November 2024 through
**Initiative 83**. It applies to the 2026 Democratic Primary.

## Tips for using RCV

- **Rank as many candidates as you want.** You don't have to rank all of them.
- **Do not rank candidates you would be unhappy to see win.** If a candidate is ranked at all,
  your vote could eventually go to them if everyone ranked above them is eliminated.
- **Your first choice is still your first choice.** Ranking a second or third candidate does
  not hurt your first-choice candidate.
- **Organizations like Free DC publish "do not rank" advisories** for certain candidates.
  These advisories are displayed on each candidate's profile page on this site.
