# About
**Kenyan R. McDuffie** is a fourth-generation Washingtonian, public servant, and proven leader who has dedicated his life to fighting for and delivering results for the residents of the District of Columbia.

Raised in the Stronghold neighborhood of Ward 5, Kenyan was shaped by a tight-knit, working-class community that instilled in him a deep belief in opportunity, accountability, and service. His family’s deep history in D.C. instilled an unshakable commitment to fairness, economic opportunity, and justice for every resident in every neighborhood.

After graduating from Jackson Reed High (formerly Wilson High), Kenyan worked for the United States Postal Service. Kenyan, using the money he earned from delivering mail to DC residents to put himself though UDC, eventually attending and graduating from Howard University and the University of Maryland School of Law. After law school, Kenyan began his legal career as a prosecutor. He later served in the U.S. Department of Justice under President Obama, working to protect and uphold individuals’ civil rights. His career in law was driven by one principle: holding bad actors accountable and delivering justice to victims.

In 2012, Kenyan was first elected to the D.C. Council, representing Ward 5, and then elected citywide in 2022. For more than 13 years on the D.C. Council, he has built a record of turning bold ideas into law and delivering tangible results for District residents.

Kenyan’s [**legislative record**](https://kenyanmcduffie.com/record) has helped make neighborhoods across the city more affordable, safer, and connected to the city’s prosperous growth. He has authored and passed landmark laws to create and preserve affordable housing, protect tenants and workers, invest in public safety rooted in accountability and prevention, and close racial and economic gaps. From securing hundreds of millions of dollars for housing and environmental justice to standing up for seniors, small businesses, and working families, Kenyan has consistently turned values into action.

He’s running for Mayor to fight for and deliver a city that works for everyone: affordable for residents from every walk of life; safer and full of opportunity for youth, adults, seniors and small business owners alike; and built to help multigenerational families thrive while supporting entrepreneurs and innovation that help our city grow. He believes Washington can be more affordable, more equitable, and safer with leadership willing to roll up its sleeves and do the hard work to get it done.

Kenyan is raising his two daughters with his wife in Ward 5 and remains deeply connected to the community that raised him.

## Housing
### More Housing, Lower Costs, Expand Homeownership

### A Comprehensive Housing Strategy To Advance Equitable Growth And Affordability For All District Residents
Housing is the single largest expense for most District families, with median rents exceeding $2,000 per month and median home prices approaching $700,000. Without decisive action, rising costs will continue to push out the very teachers, first responders, young professionals, and working families and seniors who have built their lives here or want to plant roots and make DC a permanent home.

As Councilmember, Kenyan McDuffie authored pioneering laws and prioritized equitable funding in the District’s budget to expand pathways to homeownership, strengthen affordable housing programs, and push the District to build more homes to meet the growing demand. As Mayor, Kenyan will build on this foundation, implementing **Homes for Every Generation**, a comprehensive housing strategy that both increases housing supply and protects affordability, so that DC remains a city where residents of all incomes can live, work, and raise families.

Through **Homes for Every Generation**, Kenyan will make the District the most accessible city in the region for homeownership, by dramatically increasing housing production, preserving affordable housing at risk of loss, and giving families of all sizes the stability to remain in the neighborhoods they call home.

### Making DC The Easiest Place In The Region To Build Housing
Housing shortages drive up costs. In the Washington region, experts estimate the area needs at least 320,000 additional homes by 2030 to keep up with population growth and demand. But regulatory delays and permitting barriers can add years to development timelines and millions in costs. As Mayor, Kenyan will accelerate housing expansion by:
#### Accelerating Development Approval
Creating a one-stop permitting and development interagency office and portal with a tracking dashboard to provide a single point of entry for all development services and accelerate approval.
#### Establishing A Housing Ombudsman
Establishing a Housing Ombudsman that reports to the Mayor and serves as a single point of accountability for development, troubleshoots permitting bottlenecks in real time, and ensures all housing agencies are held accountable and on track to meet clear timelines.
#### Implementing Clear Timelines
Instituting and implementing clear review and approval timelines, providing predictability, streamlining regulatory approvals across agencies and reducing redundancy that slow housing construction.
#### Modernizing Zoning Rules
Modernizing zoning rules to eliminate lengthy discretionary review, parking and other requirements that are not necessary, particularly in transit-rich neighborhoods.
#### Converting Vacant Federal Buildings
Accelerating conversion of vacant federal office buildings to housing and mixed-use by pre-entitling sites and providing clear guidance to buyers and developers.
### Protecting And Expanding Deeply Affordable Housing
Nearly 40% of DC renters are cost-burdened, meaning they spend more than 30% of their income on housing. Preserving affordable housing, particularly for residents earning below 30–60% of Area Median Income (AMI), is essential to preventing displacement. DC already invests more than $100 million annually in affordable housing through the Housing Production Trust Fund, one of the largest per-capita investments in the country. To maximize that investment, the District must deploy resources strategically to produce more units and preserve those at risk of being lost, while also reducing lease-up periods for tenants that are significantly delayed by income certification and other compliance requirements. As Mayor, Kenyan will preserve and expand DC’s affordable housing stock by:
#### Establishing "Cut The Cost" Task Force
Establishing the “Cut the Cost” Task Force, dedicated to identifying all the cost drivers that are making affordable housing projects overly expensive, with a commitment to enact its recommended reforms.
#### Accelerating Development
Accelerating deeply affordable housing developments through by-right zoning offerings in all contexts at appropriate intensities.
#### Providing Resources For Mission-Driven Providers
Providing financial resources and targeted tax abatements for mission-driven housing providers that preserve and expand deeply affordable housing, with enforceable community benefits throughout the full duration of the tax relief and annual public reporting requirements to track performance and ensure continued eligibility.
#### Deploying HPTF Dollars Efficiently
Ensuring HPTF dollars are deployed efficiently to maximize the number of affordable homes produced by administering stronger scoring for projects that produce more units per dollar, incorporating minimum private or tax-credit equity share requirements, improving coordination with federal tax credits and incentives and standardizing and accelerating underwriting and approvals.
#### Preserving Existing Affordable Housing
Establishing purpose built preservation funding mechanism that is specifically created to provide financing tools that support preservation of existing affordable housing to ensure that affordable housing covenants and existing affordable units are not lost to the market.

## Every Child, Every Step
### Investing In Our Children From Day One To College, Vocation, Career, Entrepreneurship, Homeownership
Washington, DC is a city full of opportunity, yet too many families still face barriers that make it harder for children to reach their full potential. Child care costs strain household budgets. Access to afterschool programs and enrichment opportunities remains uneven across neighborhoods. And far more young people than should ever be the case graduate without the resources or a clear next step into higher education, skilled trades, entrepreneurship, or stable work.

These challenges are connected. Addressing them requires more than isolated programs. It requires building a system that supports families early, strengthens learning and mental development  throughout childhood, and connects young people to real economic opportunity.

That is the principle behind Kenyan McDuffie’s **Every Child, Every Step** agenda. It supports families from the earliest years through high school and into adulthood with real options and real opportunity.

As Mayor, **Kenyan McDuffie** will build a seamless pipeline to success from birth to adulthood: Build wealth through Baby Bonds → Lower family costs early → Improve education and social and emotional development outcomes and expand extracurricular time → Cultivate pride and purpose→ Connect youth to career pathways.

But when we invest in a child from the beginning, the returns are outstanding. For example, research shows that every dollar spent on high-quality Early Care and Education (ECE) yields at least $3 in estimated cost savings through increased productivity and future tax revenues. 

Successful programs need to be piloted and replicated to ensure every child has the health care, family support, and early learning needed for strong mental and physical development, regardless of ward or income.  Childhood development and literacy should focus on rich language interactions, responsive caregiving, and brain‑development‑aligned stimulation in home‑ and center‑based options and limiting “screen time” with devices.

## Safer For All D.C.
### A Plan To Reduce Crime, Prevent Violence, And Protect Every Resident
Every Washingtonian deserves to feel safe walking home, sending a child to school, riding Metro, residing in any ward and calling 911. DC has made real progress on public safety, but that progress is being tested by a recent and unacceptable surge in violence and by compounding understaffing and deficiencies across the public safety system. Police, fire, emergency communications, violence prevention, reentry, and victim-support systems are all strained at once. When one part of that system breaks down, every neighborhood is less safe.

Kenyan McDuffie, a former prosecutor and trial attorney in the Department of Justice’s Civil Rights Division under President Obama, has spent his career confronting the root causes of violence and building a public-safety system that pairs accountability with prevention. As Chair of the Council’s Judiciary Committee, he advanced a public-health approach to violence prevention, comprehensive juvenile justice reform to reduce the school-to-prison pipeline, and one of the nation’s first body-worn camera policies to strengthen transparency and accountability. He also championed sentencing reform that has helped eligible residents earn a second chance and produced a 3% recidivism rate.

As Mayor, Kenyan will make the public-safety system work again: rebuilding depleted first responder ranks, modernizing 911 infrastructure, scaling violence prevention, holding agencies accountable for response failures, protecting victims and witnesses, defending immigrant families from federal overreach, and strengthening reentry so returning citizens can rebuild their lives.

Safer for ALL DC is built on five connected prongs: prevent violence before it happens, hold the public-safety system accountable, support first responders, prepare DC for every emergency, and protect every resident’s ability to call for help without fear.

This plan moves beyond single-solution politics. It combines evidence-based policing, public health, youth engagement, first-responder staffing, emergency readiness, victim protection, and civil-rights safeguards into one operational strategy for safer neighborhoods in all eight wards.

Washington’s young people deserve both accountability and opportunity. Right now, they are getting neither.

**35 Days Without Action**

On April 21, the Council postponed a vote on emergency legislation that would have preserved
the District’s curfew authority because it became clear there were not enough votes to pass the measure. Then, on May 5, the Council withdrew emergency curfew legislation after it became clear the votes were still not there. Although the Council approved permanent legislation, it cannot take effect until the congressional review period concludes, likely well into the summer months. Mayor Bowser has used her emergency authority to bridge the gap through June 6, but a 15-day executive patch is not governance. It is delay. The Council has the authority to act and should.

Extended juvenile curfew zones were effective last summer. They gave law enforcement a targeted, geographically bounded tool to intervene before dangerous situations escalated, and they showed parents and communities that their concerns would be met with decisive action, not indifference. Allowing that authority to lapse, and then offering 30 additional minutes at recreation centers, as Councilmember Lewis George proposes, does not reflect the urgency this moment demands.

I want to be direct: curfew authority, used responsibly, is a temporary and targeted public safety tool. It is not a punishment. It is not a substitute for investment. But when teen takeovers threaten the safety of residents and the young people themselves, the Council cannot afford to leave law enforcement and communities without every appropriate tool at their disposal.

**The Stakes for Home Rule Are Real**

This is not only a public safety matter. It is a Home Rule matter.

Local residents and elected officials know DC best and do not need federal intervention to keep our city safe and invest in our youth. However, U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro has spent months making the national argument that the District cannot govern itself. President Donald Trump has deployed the National Guard on DC streets and floated proposals to try 14-year-olds as adults. Every week that this Council allows curfew authority to lapse, it hands the White House and its allies fresh evidence for that narrative and justification for federal intervention in our local affairs.

DC residents elected this Council to govern. Governing means making tough decisions, not deferring them.

**Curfews Must Be Paired With Real Investment in Youth**

Enforcement alone is not a youth safety strategy. I know this because I have spent my career building the alternative.

As a member of the Council, I authored the Neighborhood Engagement Achieves Results (NEAR) Act, one of the most comprehensive public health approaches to violence prevention enacted by any American city. I championed landmark juvenile justice reform, authored one of the nation’s first body-worn camera laws, and funded Safe Passage so that students could get to and from school without harm. These were laws that enhanced public safety.

That same commitment must guide the Council’s action today. Emergency legislation should
include not only curfew zone authority, but a direct investment in our youth that expands:
- after-school and evening programming at recreation centers, with extended hours that give young people somewhere safe to be;
- summer jobs and workforce development opportunities;
- community-based mentorship through proven organizations already working in neighborhoods most affected by crime and violence; and 
- safe late-night spaces at community hubs, so that the choice for a young person on a Friday night is not between a teen takeover and nothing.

Youth disorder does not emerge from nowhere. It emerges from frustration, disconnection, lack of safety, and a sense that no one is watching out for you. DC government’s answer to that must be: we see you, we invest in you, and we also expect safety and accountability from everyone.

**A Call to Act**

I urge the Council to pass emergency legislation that reinstates curfew zone authority at its next legislative meeting. Pair it with real dollars for programming, mentorship, jobs, and safe spaces. Do it now, not after another incident, not after the summer begins, and not after federal intervention makes the decision for you.

Sincerely,

#### PRONG 1: Prevent Crime With Data, Public Health, And Community Trust
DC’s public safety system needs a more disciplined, data-driven foundation and stronger community trust. Better technology, better training, and real institutional support for officers must be paired with prevention strategies that reach residents before harm occurs. The Neighborhood Engagement Achieves Results (NEAR) Act remains underfunded, its community-policing reporting requirements haven’t been unmet, and Safe Passage is still dependent on one-time federal dollars that are running out. Kenyan will pair strong law enforcement with sustained investments in violence prevention, education, economic opportunity, and safe routes to school. As Mayor, Kenyan will:
##### Strengthen Evidence-Based Policing.
Continue modernizing MPD’s Real-Time Crime Center with the staffing, technology, camera integration, and crime-fighting tools needed to track crime patterns, support investigations, and stop violence sooner, while ensuring MPD has the training and community partnerships needed to use those tools effectively.
##### Get Illegal Guns Off The Street.
Create a stronger illegal-gun strategy using MPD, ATF, NIBIN ballistics, gun tracing, federal prosecution, community tips, and regional partnerships to identify who is carrying illegal guns, stop the next shooting, crack down on ghost guns and switches, and disrupt the trafficking networks bringing firearms into DC.
##### Fully Implement The NEAR Act’s Public Health Vision.
Stand up the Office of Violence Prevention and Health Equity within DC Health and connect residents at highest risk of violence with trauma-informed care, hospital-based intervention, mental health services, family stabilization, and wraparound support before harm escalates.
##### Continue And Bolster Safe Passage.
Identify dedicated local dollars to sustain and expand Safe Passage, placing more trained, trusted adults along student travel routes to prevent conflict, de-escalate incidents, support safe arrivals, and provide every school that requests help with qualified personnel.
##### Integrate Public Health And Violence Interruption Into Public Safety.
Build stronger partnerships between MPD, violence interrupters, schools, and community-based organizations so DC can deploy enforcement where necessary and prevention wherever possible.
##### Support Violence Prevention Work And Hold It Accountable.
Strengthen violence interruption and public-health prevention programs with clear standards, better training, real oversight, transparent performance metrics, and consequences when organizations misuse public funds or fail to deliver.

#### PRONG 2: Invest In Prevention, Intervention, And Accountability
Public safety requires a strategy that is immediate, balanced, and built to last. The District must respond quickly when violence, vandalism, or disorder threatens neighborhoods, while also addressing the conditions that allow crime to take root. That means giving young people safe places to go, ensuring serious offenses are investigated and prosecuted effectively, supporting victims and survivors, and helping returning residents rebuild their lives. As Mayor, Kenyan will:
##### Launch A Youth Engagement Strategy (YES).
Consistent with [Every Child, Every Step](https://kenyanmcduffie.com/platform/every-child), deliver targeted intervention for youth at greatest risk, expand after-school and evening programming in safe spaces, scale youth sports and mentorship citywide, and provide paid jobs, internships, and workforce pathways for DC youth.
##### Use Temporary Curfews Only As Part Of A Broader Youth Safety Strategy.
Maintain narrowly tailored, temporary curfews where needed to address violence or disorder, while investing in permanent solutions: safe spaces, mentorship, jobs, recreation, and family stabilization.
##### Strengthen Smart Prosecution Coordination.
Work with the Attorney General, U.S. Attorney’s Office, MPD, and community partners to prioritize serious violent offenses, improve case preparation, reduce repeat offending, and use data to ensure accountability where it matters most.
##### Protect Victims And Survivors.
Building on Kenyan’s convening of the Sexual Assault Victim Rights Task Force, expand funding for victim advocacy services, trauma counseling, and legal support citywide, and ensure survivors’ voices shape policy.
##### Reduce Recidivism.
Strengthen reentry programs that provide job training, housing assistance, mental health support, and family stabilization for returning residents, with a focus on interventions proven to reduce repeat offenses and make communities safer.

#### PRONG 3: Support Our First Responders
Our city’s first responders are stretched thin, and the staffing crisis now spans multiple public safety agencies. DC is losing ground in the regional and national recruitment fight: Arlington County offers a starting police salary nearly 20% higher than MPD’s, federal law enforcement agencies offer signing bonuses as high as $70,000 to $75,000 compared with DC’s $25,000 bonus, and MPD has fallen from more than 4,000 officers in 2013 to fewer than 3,200 today, costing DC more than $100 million annually in overtime. The strain extends beyond MPD. OUC still has dozens of vacant call-taker positions, and Fire and EMS spent more than $50 million on overtime last year because it lacks the personnel to fully staff units. Behind each number are public safety professionals who deserve better pay, stronger support, and a city government committed to their wellbeing. As Mayor, Kenyan will:
##### Strengthen Cadet Programs.
Create clear pathways for DC students to become police officers, firefighters, paramedics, 911 call-takers, dispatchers, and emergency-management professionals through early career exposure, paid internships, CTE partnerships, mentorship, and certification support.
##### Recruit Top Talent.
Double signing bonuses for MPD, Fire and EMS, and OUC to keep pace with surrounding jurisdictions and the federal government; improve competitive compensation; and, consistent with Homes for Every Generation, launch “Homes for DC Heroes” to help first responders become homeowners in the city they protect.
##### Improve Promotions And Retention.
Keep experienced workers in understaffed public-safety agencies by creating clearer promotion pathways, targeted retention bonuses for hard-to-fill roles, tuition and student-loan assistance, lateral-entry opportunities, and specialty pay for high-need assignments.
##### Grow The Force The Right Way.
Implement a credible pipeline strategy to add 1,000 officers over four years by cutting annual attrition from roughly 300 to 150 and expanding hiring capacity to 400 officers per year, while pairing growth with stronger community policing, real accountability, and investments in officer morale.

#### PRONG 4: Prepare D.C. For Every Emergency
Public safety does not end with crime prevention. It also means a city government that can respond when streets flood, extreme heat threatens seniors, the power goes out, or a major storm tests every agency at once. Recent emergencies have exposed gaps in equipment, coordination, communication, and after-action transparency. When the January 2026 “snowcrete” storm hit, DC lacked the equipment and interagency coordination to respond effectively. Residential streets in several wards went uncleared for nearly a week, and of the eight AlertDC messages sent during the storm, only one addressed snow removal. The summer of 2025 then brought flash flooding, Metro disruptions, and extreme heat events that further strained the city’s response capacity. The District must have the resources, staffing, communications systems, and continuity planning to endure and recover from any hazard while protecting seniors, people with disabilities, unhoused residents, and other vulnerable Washingtonians. As Mayor, Kenyan will:
##### Modernize 911 Infrastructure.
Strengthen the Unified Communications Center’s technology, backup power, and system redundancies to reduce outages and keep emergency response online when residents need it most.
##### Require Regular Cross-Agency Emergency Drills.
Prioritize training and exercises for all executive departments in joint environments such as the District’s Emergency Operations Center so every agency knows its role and can respond operationally and tactically to no-notice or quickly developing emergencies.
##### Push The Federal Government To Honor Its Commitments.
Hold the federal administration accountable to provide necessary funding for HSEMA and other mission-critical District departments.
##### Double Down On Resident Emergency Preparedness.
Partner with civic associations, advisory neighborhood commissioners, and community groups to deliver neighborhood-level training; improve public communications during and after emergencies; and require transparent performance metrics and after-action reviews.

#### PRONG 5: Protect Residents From Federal Immigration Overreach
At a time when federal immigration enforcement is spreading fear in DC neighborhoods, Kenyan will keep MPD focused on its core mission: protecting and serving all residents. When families are afraid that calling 911 could lead to deportation, witnesses stay silent, victims go unprotected, and every neighborhood becomes less safe. Public safety depends on trust, and every Washingtonian, regardless of immigration status, should be able to call for help, report a crime, and seek justice without fear. As Mayor, Kenyan will:
##### End MPD’s Cooperation With ICE On Day One.
Prohibit DC police from acting as federal immigration agents or sharing residents’ immigration status with ICE.
##### Require Transparency Around Federal Enforcement.
Demand formal notification and reporting protocols so federal law enforcement agencies provide timely notice to designated DC authorities when conducting enforcement operations in District neighborhoods.
##### Guarantee Legal Representation For Immigrant Families.
Provide civil right-to-counsel protections so immigrant residents facing deportation are not forced to navigate the federal system alone.

## Education
### Supporting Families Preparing Futures
### Affordability
#### Centralized Family Support Portal
Parents should not need to take a day off work to figure out how to enroll in programs, request supports, or resolve a school-related problem. Families regularly describe access to youth programs and student services as confusing, cost- and time-consuming, and issues linger when deadlines are missed or responsibilities bounce across agencies. 

As Mayor, Kenyan will prioritize education-adjacent supports so families can start requests in one place, receive a trackable case number, see clear timelines, and escalate when agencies miss deadlines. This makes support easier to access, faster to deliver, and simpler to hold accountable.
#### **Transportation**
Program access is not meaningful if students cannot reliably get to school, out-of-school (OST) sites, internships, career programs, or credential training. Transportation barriers can drive missed participation and missed work time for families. 

As Mayor, Kenyan will treat mobility as part of access so students can actually participate in the opportunities the city funds, including OST programming and career pathways.
#### **Universal Out-Of-School Time**
Working parents need reliable coverage after school and in the summer, and many families rely on out-of-school time as their primary childcare. DC already has infrastructure through Learn24 and the OST Office that can be expanded and coordinated to improve equitable access and quality. 

As Mayor, and consistent with [Every Child, Every Step](https://kenyanmcduffie.com/platform/every-child), Kenyan will stabilize and expand OST so families are not navigating a patchwork of waitlists and inconsistent schedules, and so _every school is a place families can count on for out-of-school opportunities._ This makes access simpler, more predictable, and available across wards.
### Opportunity

#### Paid Work-Based Learning And Apprenticeships
DC needs more “learn-and-earn” options that connect training directly to hiring. These programs should translate into stable jobs, higher wages, and long-term economic mobility.

As Mayor, Kenyan will scale paid internships, apprenticeships, and direct-to-work programming so young people have the option to move from either a high school or postsecondary education to a good job with fewer barriers, and so employers have a stronger reason to hire DC talent. This includes taking DC’s Summer Youth Employment Program to the next level as a stronger bridge into high-quality placements that connect to career pathways, and expanding work-based learning tied to high-demand sectors.
#### Career Pathways
Students deserve strong career pathways no matter what they plan to do after high school. The District should build clear, citywide pathways to both higher education and family-sustaining employment by aligning programs with growing industries and good-paying jobs, including clean energy, skilled trades, technology, health care, and other high-demand fields.

As Mayor, Kenyan will expand and strengthen Career Technical Education programs, Advanced Technical Centers, and career academies aligned with high-demand sectors so students graduate with real skills, industry-recognized credentials, and direct connections to employers.
#### Adult Education Programs
Adult education and workforce programs should be measured by results, not just enrollment. DC should lead in building a homegrown talent pipeline that connects residents to good jobs in industries that are emerging, growing and hiring here.

As Mayor, Kenyan will align adult education pathways and UDC workforce programs with labor market demand, while tracking completion, credential attainment, and job placement so public investments translate into real opportunity. That means stronger pathways for residents who want to upskill, reskill, or earn a credential connected to real hiring.
### Accountability
#### Classroom Quality And Clear Expectations
Quality should not depend on ward, school type, or luck. Every child in DC deserves a strong foundation in literacy and math, high expectations in every classroom, and a school system that prepares them for the world they are entering, not the one of the past. Families should be able to trust that no matter where their child learns, a DC diploma reflects real learning, real skills, and real readiness for college, career, and life.

As Mayor, Kenyan will prioritize equal funding for DCPS and public charter schools because all students deserve quality schools. Kenyan will also ensure schools are preparing students for a modern world by supporting responsible use of new tools, including AI, while protecting academic integrity and ensuring students build real skills.
#### Special Education, Mental Health And School Health
Families are tired of hearing excuses when it comes to DC failing to deliver quality education and health services on time. While legal timelines and requirements already exist, too many students and families still experience delays, missed services and inconsistent support. 

As Mayor, Kenyan will ensure DC’s education agencies and student-serving systems deliver on the timelines already required by law by building the capacity to meet demand, using rapid service recovery when deadlines are missed, and making it easier for families to get issues resolved without navigating a maze. This includes responsive special education services, reliable school-based mental health access, and dependable school health coverage so support show up when students need them.
#### Responsible Technology And Screen Time Standards
Research on screen time and child development is growing, and DC families deserve schools that take those findings seriously. Families should have full transparency into technology use, explicit consent rights over data-collecting apps, and clear opt-out pathways with low-tech alternatives and no penalty.

As Mayor, Kenyan will establish clear, age-appropriate standards for device use across DC schools. Kenyan will eliminate all device use for Pre-K through 2nd grade and move grades 3 through 8 to shared computer cart or lab access used only when curriculum warrants it. Student access to YouTube, gaming, video-streaming, and social media will be blocked on all school-issued devices. All instructional apps will be required to be ad-free and meet independent efficacy standards. Generative AI tools will be disabled on school-issued devices until proven safe, legal, and effective. Kenyan will also enforce the bell-to-bell ban on cell phones in DC Public Schools and push to expand that ban to DC Charter schools. Screen time will not be used as a reward or behavioral reinforcement.
#### Dashboards, Attendance And Assessment Clarity
Families deserve clear, parent-friendly information about what schools are delivering and how students are doing. Results should be understandable, trackable, and tied to action when performance falls short, enabling schools and agencies to coordinate supports that help students, teachers and families.

As Mayor, Kenyan will make performance information easier to understand and more useful, including clearer explanations of assessments and what results mean for students and schools. Kenyan will also center chronic absenteeism as a measurable priority by setting clear goals and publicly tracking progress.
