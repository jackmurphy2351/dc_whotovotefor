# People-First Platform

Janeese Lewis George is running for DC mayor because she knows DC is too expensive, from housing to childcare. She too has felt the squeeze and is ready to do something about it. She knows that we can tackle the problems facing DC residents by strategically and effectively utilizing all that the government can offer and do. Lewis George will work with anyone to **improve the lives of the people in DC**, and she will **stand up to anyone** who does harm to our residents.

# Childcare For All

DC has the highest childcare costs in the nation. At the same time, many childcare workers
earn poverty wages. Implementing universal affordable childcare will help thousands of
families and workers manage their costs at a time of rising prices, and provide every DC child
with a strong start in life.
**The Plan**: We’re already halfway to universal affordable childcare through existing subsidy and workforce programs. Janeese Lewis George will build on what’s already working. Her plan has
three components:
### Cover More Families
- Every family will be eligible for childcare assistance and families will spend no more than 7% of their household income on childcare, with many families paying less and some paying nothing.
- That means if your family is earning less than $100,000 a year, you will spend no more than $600 a month on childcare.
- Janeese will ensure costs stay low by expanding the childcare subsidy to every DC family over a phased timeline. The subsidy will be funded by federal and local dollars. Janeese will prioritize funding for universal affordable childcare in her annual budgets and consider closing tax loopholes to fund universal affordable childcare.
- Families will still apply and choose their childcare setting: licensed centers, family childcare homes, or registered Family, Friend, and Neighbor (FFN) Care.
### Increase Childcare Supply
- Janeese will boost investments in FFN Care and provide microgrants to skill up these caregivers without forcing them into center-based licensing systems.
- Janeese will co-locate infant and toddler programs in DC Public Schools (DCPS) and public charter school buildings with excess capacity, as well as in underutilized District-owned facilities.
- A dedicated facilities grant fund will allow providers to purchase property or build out current space, prioritizing neighborhoods with the greatest unmet need.
### Invest In Workforce
- Janeese will build on the success of the Early Childhood Educator Pay Equity Fund to guarantee wages and benefits that are comparable to those of DCPS teachers covered by union contract.
- Janeese will continue to partner with our unions to create good, safe jobs.
- Janeese will work to maximize enrollment in affordable early childhood education degree and certification programs, allowing different entry points for people with different starting points.

**We Can Get This Done**: DC proved that bold investments work by successfully implementing universal Pre-K 3 and Pre-K 4 and the Pay Equity Fund. Now DC must lead again by adopting
universal affordable childcare.

# Affordable Utilities For All

All Washingtonians should be able to afford everyday needs like electricity and other utilities. But working people across the District have seen their Pepco and other utility bills skyrocket, with some people’s costs doubling or even tripling in recent months. High demand from AI data centers and inefficient infrastructure have contributed to high costs. But another significant cause is DC’s failure to provide proper oversight of Pepco as the company has increased rates each of the last three years, much faster than it has in other cities and states. That’s money that should be in the pockets of DC residents, not Pepco’s investors. Recently, the courts held DC’s Public Service Commission responsible for failing to look out for us in approving Pepco’s utility price increases.

**As mayor, Janeese will lower our costs by**:
- Protecting Washingtonians from Pepco and Washington Gas Rate Hikes
- Holding AI Data Centers Accountable
- Increasing Access to Affordable Energy Tools
- Removing Barriers to Energy Assistance for People Who Need It
- Stopping Energy Scams and Exploitation
### The 10-point plan

#### 1. Protect Washingtonians from Pepco and Washington Gas rate hikes.
DC controls the profits of utility companies, and right now utilities are charging more than they need to, which means residents and businesses are paying too much, all to benefit investors. It doesn’t have to be this way. Janeese will appoint Public Service Commissioners who will always center community impact in approving or disapproving rates. This will put money back in our pockets while still allowing utilities to make the investments they need to modernize our grid and maintain reliability.
#### 2. Use regional cooperation and legal strategies to hold AI data centers accountable.
AI data centers in Virginia are driving up our costs and we aren’t using all our tools to stop them. As mayor, Janeese will work closely with governors whose constituents are facing the same painful consequences of these AI data centers. She will also advance local and regional legal strategies.
#### 3. Expand community solar, government solar, and balcony solar to cut electric bills.
Janeese will substantially expand community solar on all available government buildings, school parking lots, playgrounds, and on vacant government land to generate solar energy credits. She will also provide utility credits to reduce low and middle-income households’ power bills. We can do this cost-effectively by fully implementing and expanding the Solar Shade Expansion Amendment Act.

We can reduce government energy bills by using smart contracts to add solar on DC-owned properties. Implementing energy savings performance contracts (ESPCs) will make the District’s schools and offices more comfortable, healthier, and more efficient, following the law to move to net zero. This will free up funding to be put to use for District residents to help them invest in energy efficiency. But we should also provide more options for renters and those in buildings who can’t install solar on their roofs. By adding incentive programs for residents to buy balcony solar systems and making it easier to get them up and running, we can make renting more affordable, strengthen our grid, and reduce the cost of electricity for everyone.
#### 4. Empower consumers through utility rate options.
Janeese will give people the ability to opt in to lower rates at certain times of the day. This will help households (especially those with electric vehicles) save money through technologies like home battery storage. This will also reduce peak power demand and create savings for everyone.
#### 5. Support smarter EV charging.
As EV adoption expands, Janeese will approach charging the right way so everyone saves money. Most of DC’s battery capacity is in electric vehicles. So Janeese will incentivize people to charge at night, when we have surplus energy, and give them the option to return energy during the day, when it’s most needed. EV owners and everyone else will benefit.
#### 6. Focus *Project Pipes* on safety, not profit.
The current Washington Gas plan charges our households at least $150M to add expensive new pipes that won’t be used for very long. That will prolong our reliance on dangerous gas in homes and will dramatically increase costs for households that use gas. The current plan isn’t optimized for safety, and Janeese will insist that we do better.
#### 7. Bring down utility bills and help those who fall behind.
Janeese will reduce bills for low-income households to ensure everyone has access to utilities they can afford. She will substantially increase automatic enrollment in utility discount programs; expand energy efficiency upgrades for low-income households to reduce energy use; and prioritize local funds to support federal LIHEAP to provide targeted support when households can’t pay their bills.
#### 8. Allow customers to access energy-saving tools even if they don’t have the money upfront.
Janeese will work toward requiring Pepco to implement on bill financing, which allows customers to upgrade their homes to save money through energy efficiency without upfront costs. This approach, proven in other states, can help households of all income levels invest in energy savings that pays for itself. We should also expand the work the DC Sustainable Energy Utility does to bring energy savings to District businesses and residents. Both these improvements will help households save money for themselves but also help everyone else– energy conservation lowers the price for everyone.
#### 9. Save small businesses money on their utility bills.
Most large companies in the District buy low-cost power, saving nearly $200 million per year – but small businesses don’t have the time to shop around for energy. Janeese will establish bulk power purchasing and make it easier for small businesses to sign up and save.
#### 10. Protect DC residents from energy reseller scams.
Too many low-income households get their electricity from overpriced third-party energy suppliers, which scam residents into signing contracts through which they are overcharged. This raises energy bills and makes families accrue more debt. As mayor, Janeese will initiate more stringent regulation of third-party suppliers and ensure they can’t charge residents more than Pepco for the same energy.

# Economic development for A Thriving DC
DC should be a city of choice, where residents can build a career, raise a family, and become a part of the community. Our city must work for the people who live here in addition to the people who commute in for work and their employers. As mayor, Janeese will invest in reaalizing this vision of our city. She will:

### Reimagine a Thriving Downtown for All by:
- **Creating a Downtown Development Corporation** with bonding authority to finance and implement revitalization strategies and once again make DC a global destination for capital investment.
- **Fostering mixed-use development** through land use changes, federal government repositioning, and public-private partnerships that boost the housing, education, health care, entertainment, recreation, retail, sports, and hospitality sectors and ultimately reduce downtown office space to 50% or less.
- **Providing free temporary or permanent spaces** for pop-ups, incubators, and small businesses in DC-owned spaces or in vacant commercial spaces.
- **Integrating transportation infrastructure** that works for all modes and improves walkability for pedestrians – the primary users of downtown.

### Leverage the Economic Power of Universities and Hospitals by:
- **Partnering with DC’s anchor universities to build innovation districts**, standing up a council of university presidents working on converting research programs into employment opportunities.
- **Creating a global hub for higher education**, where major universities worldwide establish satellite locations for communications, research, education, and alumni affairs.
- **Developing a health innovation program** in cooperation with area hospitals to spur the growth of health-related jobs and businesses.
- **Promoting university-based retirement communities** that attract older residents to DC.

### Make Union Station the East Coast’s Premier Transit Hub by:
- **Building out Union Station redevelopment** to create a local pipeline for 40,000 construction, corporate, and commercial jobs.
- **Fostering transit-oriented housing development** through permitting and federal partnerships.
### Tackle Economic Disparities by:
- **Releasing the existing disparity study and commission a second disparity study** to document under-utilization of women- and minority led firms in contracts to help ensure economic and racial equity are prioritized.
- **Re-envisioning the Certified Business Enterprise (CBE) program** to make DC government contracts a springboard to business growth and generational economic opportunity.
- Prioritizing development that puts DC residents first, **including grocery stores and retail amenities in Wards 5, 7 and 8**.
- **Providing lease subsidies for small businesses** locating in underserved neighborhoods.

# Reliable Transportation For All

People-first transportation means fast, reliable, and safe transportation for all residents regardless of how they get around. It means a system where buses, metro lines, pedestrian walkways, and bike lanes work together to connect neighborhoods, get people to work, improve access to essential services, and serve residents in all eight wards. 

Janeese’s plan will:

**Create free transit for those who need it.** Janeese will make sure inability to pay isn’t a barrier to getting around the city. Buses will be free for DC residents enrolled in the SNAP food assistance program and she’ll explore other ways to expand financial support for transit. 

**Build a world-class bus system**. Janeese will make the buses on DC’s busiest routes faster and more reliable by adding dedicated bus lanes and optimizing bus stops. She will assess and address gaps in the bus network, including transit deserts, and require major development projects to incorporate bus station amenities that are senior friendly and consistent with high-frequency transit service. 

**Designate car-free zones.** Janeese will improve safety, spur economic activity, and animate public spaces by establishing car-free areas. She will engage residents and businesses in creating pedestrian-only streets, using the Penn Quarter Holiday Market as a model, as well as multi-use streets, like Adams Morgan’s 18th Street NW and 7th Street SE at Eastern Market, that close periodically for activities. 

**Prioritize safety.** Janeese will use proven measures to keep people safe on our roads. She will fully invest in the STEER Act to remove dangerous drivers from our streets; create clear, protected space for deliveries, short-term stops, and other essential roadside activities; and redesign dangerous intersections where high speeds and poor visibility put pedestrians at risk. 

**Expand commuter bike lanes.** Janeese will make it easier and safer for cyclists to navigate the city. She will implement the community-supported protected bike lanes, prioritize completion of the Shepherd Branch Trail in Wards 7 and 8, and create a northeast-northwest protected bike lane connecting the north-south commuter bikeways.   

**Reduce gridlock.** After Janeese said she would release the DDOT Congestion Pricing study within 48 hours of being sworn in, Mayor Bowser released the study herself – a good first step. As mayor, Janeese will carefully review and if necessary update the study and work with community members, District officials, and officials from surrounding jurisdictions to consider implementation.

**Provide dedicated funding for Metro**. Janeese will create certainty for residents and businesses by working with surrounding jurisdictions to maintain sufficient, dedicated, long-term funding for a robust metro system.

# Safe communities for all
Keeping everyone who lives, works, or visits DC safe is nonnegotiable. Safe communities keep families here, help children thrive, create opportunity, and improve quality of life for everyone. As a former prosecutor, I understand what drives violence in our city and how to address it. We must respond to crime by holding offenders accountable, while also investing in proven prevention and intervention strategies that disrupt cycles of violence. We must step in early with high-risk individuals before a crisis occurs. We must also fix District agencies that are critical to public safety, like our 911 call center and juvenile justice agency.

As a Councilmember, I worked block-by-block to implement a comprehensive public safety strategy that balances enforcement with intervention, and prevention mechanisms. As Mayor, I will deploy our resources strategically to further drive down crime, restore community trust, improve agency performance, and prevent crime before it happens.

To make DC safer, I will:
### Enforce our laws and support law enforcement agencies by:
- Removing illegal guns off our streets and holding those who traffic or wield guns in our communities accountable.
- Strengthening and expanding resources to detectives to aid in solving more crimes
- Launching training on constitutional stops to build stronger court cases and rebuild community trust with MPD.
- Addressing MPD’s vacancy and retention crisis by expanding the police cadet program, reducing forced overtime, renovating aging facilities, and providing child care benefits that make the District competitive with other local and federal police departments.
- Enhancing 911 by addressing personnel absenteeism, implementing comprehensive training, and strengthening quality assurance processes with improved technology.
- Strengthening DC’s crime lab by increasing capacity to clear backlogs faster and enhance oversight over accreditation standards.
- Expanding access to adequate resources to heal for victims of violent crimes and survivors.
### Drive down youth crime by:
- Addressing chronic school absenteeism throughout the District by streamlining anti-truancy efforts into one agency and expanding home visiting.
- Ensuring special education, multilingual, and school-based mental health services are resourced to address the needs of students and families.
- Expanding afterschool programs citywide and investing in more safe spaces and activities for teenagers.
- Providing year-round youth employment opportunities, including expanding options for training and workforce programs to set youth up with post-secondary employment.
- Reforming our juvenile justice agency—the Department of Youth Rehabilitation Services—to deliver better outcomes for young people in its custody.
### Intervene when residents need the most support by:
- Empowering crisis responders and other mental and behavioral health professionals to reduce police workloads and ensure that residents in crisis receive appropriate responses.
- Expanding economic opportunities for returning citizens and other residents affected by the criminal justice system.
- Reengaging disconnected young people with education, workforce development, and job opportunities.
- Supporting effective programs like the Pathways Program, ONSE Leadership Academy, and Safe Passage.
- Ending all cooperation with ICE to restore trust between communities and District government and ensure law enforcement can focus on deterring crime.
### Prevent violence before it occurs by:
- Addressing gun violence through a public health approach and strengthening violence prevention programs.
- Creating community hubs that provide access to government services across all 8 Wards.
- Improving interagency collaboration to remove siloes between government agencies.
- Deepening investments where they matter most: mental and behavioral health services, educational opportunities for disengaged residents, and meaningful pathways to employment opportunities.
- Ensuring DC residents have access to safe housing as a source of stability in their lives, including by funding transitional housing programs for emerging adults and returning citizens and housing relocation assistance for victim families.
- Making our streets and intersections safer for all by improving traffic safety infrastructure and holding repeat dangerous drivers accountable.

# Making it Easier to Live, Own, and Build for all of DC
Stable housing is the foundation of strong families and well-resourced communities. But longtime residents are being priced out of the District, and young families cannot afford to put down roots.

As Mayor, Janeese will lower costs for renters and homeowners while making it easier for developers to build the housing to meet the needs of all.
### Protect tenants
- **Stabilizing rent** by modernizing rent stabilization so tenants have predictable housing costs and housing providers have clear guidelines.
- **Ending the slumlord business model** by increasing inspections and enforcement to identify substandard housing and requiring rapid remediation of unsafe conditions and licensure sanctions.
- **Creating a one-stop affordable housing system** that establishes an Affordable Housing Delivery Team to coordinate agencies (including DHCD, DCHFA, DOB, and DDOT) and utility providers so that housing projects move efficiently from concept to completion.
### Expand homeownership
- **Increasing down payment assistance** by fully funding programs (DC OpenDoors, HPAP, EPAP) that help residents buy homes and by exploring innovative tools to lower borrowing costs.
- **Protecting longtime homeowners** by expanding foreclosure prevention programs, providing rehabilitation assistance for seniors, and strengthening protections against predatory reverse mortgages.
- **Enhancing pathways from renting to owning** to help tenants become owners through cooperatives, tenant purchases, and structured negotiations.
- **Investing in workforce housing** to ensure that middle-income residents, including teachers, nurses, and public servants, can afford to live in the communities they serve.
### Build More Homes and Better Homes
- **Allowing for more housing citywide** by removing outdated zoning restrictions so more homes and more types of homes can be built across DC, particularly near transit corridors.
- Streamlining permitting and reduce red tape to reduce delays, eliminate unnecessary costs, and accelerate housing production.
- **Launching Dignified Homes DC** by building DC-owned mixed-income housing with stable rents, ensuring that public housing investments prioritize residents over profit.
- **Preserving affordable housing before it is lost** by creating a dedicated preservation unit and early warning system that tracks properties nearing the end of tax credit compliance periods and refinancing windows so the city can intervene before affordability disappears.
- **Treating housing with urgency** by conducting a full audit of the systems to identify the outdated processes that slow housing development and stymy growth within the first 100 days. Janeese will set clear service standards across all agencies that touch housing and development so residents, builders, and community partners know what to expect and when. The DC government will be a partner in building strong neighborhoods.
### Restore Faith in Government
- **Demanding accountability for housing investments** by tying investments to measurable outcomes and prioritizing residents with the greatest need.
- **Reforming the DC Housing Authority** by establishing a Deputy Mayor for housing, restoring resident representation on the board, and overhauling the voucher systems so taxpayers are not overpaying for substandard units.
- **Fast-tracking public benefit housing** by making accelerated plan review the default pathway for affordable housing projects–faster timelines reduce costs and stretch public dollars further.
- **Increasing transparency** by publishing clear criteria for DHCD and DCHFA funding decisions and maintaining a public pipeline tracker so residents and developers can see which housing projects are moving forward.
