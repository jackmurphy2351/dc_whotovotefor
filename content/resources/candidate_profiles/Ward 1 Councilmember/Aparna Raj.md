# About
Community organizer.  
Union member.   
Proud daughter of immigrants.   
Your Ward 1 neighbor.

Aparna Raj has called DC home for a decade. Driven by her experiences as renter, union member and queer woman of color, she is running for Council in Ward 1 to ensure that every Ward 1 constituent is able to afford to live here and contribute to our shared community for years to come.   

Like many who call our Ward home, Aparna knows how precarious it is to make ends meet. As a child, she heard her mom’s stories about being unable to take sick time as a recent immigrant to the United States, working minimum wage in retail. And when her dad lost his job in 2009, her family scrambled to figure out how to make ends meet. As her parents age, the possibility of a medical emergency throwing them into bankruptcy looms. Aparna and her husband, like many others in DC, want to be able to settle down and build a life here, but are feeling the crunch of the rising cost of living.  

> "We shouldn't have to struggle just to get by. Our leaders need to do right by the people, not billionaires."

These family experiences were eye-opening, and inspired Aparna to volunteer with the Metro DC Chapter of the Democratic Socialists of America (DSA), the largest non-union membership organization in the region. Through Metro DC DSA, Aparna has organized for tenants rights, for better conditions for workers, and against the Trump administration. Aparna has consistently and unabashedly advocated for bodily autonomy, abortion access and protections for our immigrant neighbors.  
  
Her life has been defined by her fight for everyone to have a place to call home:  Aparna has served on the ANC 1A Housing Justice and Zoning Committee, advocating to build housing that meets people’s needs, instead of prioritizing developer profits. And she’s organized tenants in Ward 1 and across DC to take on their landlords, fight slum conditions, and win rent concessions. She has taken on the real estate lobby to defend and expand tenant protections.  
  
As a union member, Aparna served on her unit’s bargaining committee to win pay increases and better benefits for her and her coworkers. She fought to raise the tipped minimum wage in DC and has been active in supporting DC’s workers, from picketing alongside restaurant workers, bus drivers, and mechanics, to rallying in defense of federal workers and all workers across the district.

She lives in Columbia Heights with her husband, Stuart, and their little dog, Frank.  
  
She’s running for office because now, more than ever, we need a Council for the many, not the few.

# Issues
A better, more affordable DC is possible. Aparna is running for Council to fight for every person’s right to a stable home; every child’s right to a vibrant, quality public education; every senior’s right to age in place; and all of our rights to afford to live, and to live freely, without fear.  
  
This future is within reach. But for too long, the Mayor and members of the Council have prioritized corporate developers and wealthy lobbyists at the expense of everyday people. We shouldn’t have to accept crumbs.   
  
Our government should work for all of us. Aparna has a plan to get us there, including **rent caps**, **free childcare for all**, and **standing up for DC.**
## Clean, Affordable Energy
Because utility companies are monopolies, the only check on sky high utility bills is government regulation. But the District’s regulators are falling short. Last summer, the average residential electric bill was [up by more than 20%](https://51st.news/dc-electricity-bill-high-pepco/) than the year before. And this year will be worse, because [Pepco hiked up our bills yet again](https://www.americanprogress.org/article/residents-of-49-states-and-washington-d-c-face-increasing-electric-and-natural-gas-bills/) in January, on top of another rate hike that hit last June—largely to subsidize [new AI data centers and Big Tech special interests](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2025-ai-data-centers-electricity-prices/). And [Washington Gas’s new rate increases](https://archive.ph/Z6hcv) went into effect this winter, driving up our utility costs even more.  As working families get squeezed, [nearly one in four DC households](https://www.wepowerdc.org/latest-news/december-pepco-numbers-as-pepco-revenue-spiked-so-did-disconnection-notices-for-dc-households) have fallen behind on their electric bills—and Pepco increasingly shuts off power to families who are struggling to pay. Meanwhile, our rising electricity bills are underwriting Silicon Valley speculation and lining the pockets of utility shareholders.  
  
Aparna will address the energy affordability crisis—starting by lowering energy bills and guaranteeing relief to DC residents. She will advocate for investing in DC’s abundant renewable energy resources, especially to insulate DC ratepayers from skyrocketing electric prices due to data center sprawl in Virginia and Maryland. And Aparna will fight to make sure that DC’s utilities comply with their legal mandate to prioritize the public interest over monopoly utility shareholders.   
  
### Lower electric bills by regulating utility monopolies
Today, the District’s utility monopolies have too much power to raise our bills with weak oversight. Aparna will take immediate action to lower energy bills in DC, including by:
- **Demand stricter oversight of the utilities and rate increases:** Because utilities like Pepco are monopolies, DC has a Public Service Commission (PSC) that is supposed to regulate utilities in the public interest. The PSC is responsible for making decisions about rate hikes or infrastructure investments. But today, the PSC is failing DC residents. The Commission has uncritically approved major rate increases that drive up household bills, all while ignoring their responsibility to advance the District’s clean energy commitments. Meanwhile, the revolving door between the PSC and for-profit utilities raises serious questions about whether the Commission is protecting the public or their future paychecks. Aparna will work to ensure that the PSC fulfills its mandate of protecting working people and the environment. She will fight to appoint Commissioners and hire staff with a demonstrated commitment to consumer protection or environmental law. Similarly, Aparna will push the PSC to effectively review utilities spending and assets thoroughly to ensure that special interests are paying their fair share and that we are not being overcharged for our energy.   
- **Reduce Pepco’s guaranteed return on investment:** Not only is Pepco a monopoly, Pepco’s investors are currently _guaranteed_ a 9.5% return on their investment by the PSC. That’s an unnecessarily high rate of return, given the low risk investors face when lending money to a monopoly utility. Aparna will fight to reduce Pepco’s margin to a more reasonable rate, such as 6%. This would immediately lower residential electric bills, while still allowing Pepco to raise capital for grid improvements when needed. 
- **Restrict corporate lobbying using ratepayer funds:** Pepco and Washington Gas [spend ratepayer dollars on lobbying](https://washingtoncitypaper.com/article/659647/washington-gas-deploys-a-mess-of-lobbyists-to-kill-legislation-promoting-electric-appliances/)—including lobbying Congress to overturn climate commitments set by the DC Council. Meanwhile, their allies are suing to overturn the District’s building efficiency code, which helps us transition to cleaner, cheaper energy. As Ward One Councilmember, Aparna will fight to stop DC's utilities from using ratepayer funds to lobby against our interests—and to return the savings to residents in the form of lower bills. 
- **Remove barriers to financial assistance programs:** DC operates several  programs to help residents lower their electric utility bills, including the Utility Discount Program, the Residential Aid Discount, and Solar for All. But DC has made it pointlessly difficult and time-consuming for residents to apply for the programs, to the extent that only [20% of eligible customers](https://x.com/maustermuhle/status/2037172041271357720) are enrolled. Aparna will work to reduce paperwork barriers in these programs, including by working to establish a single application process and improve coordination between programs so residents can quickly and easily get the help they deserve. She will also use her constituent services office to support residents in filling out applications.
### Invest in cheap, reliable, and renewable energy resources
With Big Tech data centers across the entire region sucking up power, we need more electricity than ever before to keep costs from jumping even higher. And the cheapest form of energy is clean, renewable energy we can build right in the District. By investing in DC’s ability to create our own power, we can rely less on the regional electric grid and avoid AI-fueled rate hikes. Aparna will fight to lower electricity bills while delivering on DC’s ambitious climate commitments, including by:
- **Fund solar for all:** D.C.’s Solar for All program is a national model for lowering utility bills by investing in solar energy. Aparna supports fully funding the program, which would lower bills for 100,000 DC families. She will also work to bring community solar programs to renters in Ward 1 by encouraging large, multifamily buildings to enroll in the program.  
- **Facilitate home electrification:** Aparna supports fully funding the Affordable Home Electrification Program to help residents lower utility bills by upgrading to more efficient electric appliances. When combined, these programs make it easier for residents to reduce their electric bills and access cleaner, more reliable energy. 
- **Connect solar to the grid for less:** Pepco is charging customers [prohibitive fees ranging from $2,000 to $15,000](https://opc-dc.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/OPC-Solar-Interconnection-Report.pdf) to connect their solar panels to the grid. They’ve also [mishandled metering and accounting for community solar customers in DC](https://dcist.com/story/23/07/07/pepco-dc-community-solar-meter-violation/) regarding the amount of energy their solar panels generate, preventing households from lowering their bills. Aparna supports common sense interconnection reform to keep costs down and ensure families can easily access the most affordable energy available. 
- **Promote microgrids for energy reliability:** The vast majority of DC’s energy comes from fossil fuel power plants outside of the District. It’s time we change that and generate more power locally. Aparna will support building out rooftop and balcony solar, including by expanding DC’s ongoing [virtual power plant pilot project](https://dcpsc.org/Clean-Energy/Innovations/PowerPath-DC/Virtual-Power-Plant.aspx). And she will advocate for building solar-powered microgrids that can deliver cheaper, cleaner energy to residents. 
- **Deliver on DC’s climate commitments:** The District has ambitious climate commitments, but is falling behind on implementation. As your Councilmember, Aparna will keep DC on track to meet its targets for cleaner, healthier buildings, including by defending DC’s Building Efficiency Performance Standards and Greener Government Building Act from proposed rollbacks.
### Create good jobs and protect DC’s consumers
While working to lower rates, Aparna will also fight to protect District households from scams or heavy-handed collections tactics by:
- **Secure clean energy jobs:** DC’s energy future should be union-made. Workers power our city, and building out the next generation of DC’s grid means new opportunities for reliable union work. From electric heavy ups to geothermal pilots, the District’s clean energy future is also a green union job future.   
- **Create guardrails for power and heat shutoffs:** Pepco and Washington Gas disconnect thousands of households who fall behind on their bills each year. Shutoffs only make it harder for residents to pay their bills, leaving families without power to cook, heat their homes, or apply for jobs. And then residents owe a fee just to get power back on. Aparna believes there must be more safeguards to protect residents at risk of shutoffs. As Ward 1 Councilmember, she will advocate for extending the period of the year where shutoffs are restricted due to extreme weather, and expand options for vulnerable residents—households with children, the elderly or disabled, and recipients of financial aid programs like SNAP—to access reduced fares and payment plans, rather than facing dangerous power shutoffs.
- **Protect consumers from electricity scams:** Some for-profit electricity companies in the District have an alarming track record of signing households up with [deceptive methods, misrepresenting their contracts, or aggressive marketing tactics](https://washingtoncitypaper.com/article/766102/utility-bills-energy-scam-ag/). But customers who sign those contracts end up paying 70% more on average than customers who get their electricity directly from Pepco. Aparna will use her power on the DC Council to crack down on for-profit utility scams. 

### Democratize Our Utilities
In the long run, Aparna will work to transition to a public ownership of energy utilities and how DC generates our electricity. Our current system relies on government regulators to defend consumers from investor-owned, monopoly utilities. But in practice, the Public Service Commission has not successfully operated as a check on investor-owned utilities that prioritize maximizing profits for their shareholders.   
  
Aparna believes that DC should transition to public ownership of its utilities. There are [more than 2,000 municipal utilities across the country](https://www.publicpower.org/system/files/documents/MAP-%20Where%20is%20Public%20Power.pdf), including the entire state of Nebraska. These public power customers have the lowest bills and [pay 13% less](https://www.publicpower.org/public-power/stats-and-facts) than customers of other utility types. And these utilities deliver more reliable power and good union jobs by building clean energy generation that delivers cleaner, cheaper power. In New York, for example, the [Build Public Renewables Act](https://publicpowerny.org/legislation/) is allowing the New York Power Authority to build hydropower and other clean energy that allows us to retire fossil fuels while creating tens of thousands of green union jobs.  
  
If DC wants to meet our climate commitments and lower bills, the District needs a utility we can work with, not against. Public ownership puts the city in charge of its energy future, provides greater accountability to residents, and allows the city to provide clean energy at affordable rates.
## Housing for People, Not for Profit
Ward 1’s housing stock is made up of a beautiful mix of single-family homes, group houses, and multifamily apartment buildings of all sizes, such as The Woodner, Tivoli Gardens, and the Buena Vista Co-op. This mix of housing gives our neighborhoods a distinct personality. But there still aren’t enough options for people of all income levels and family sizes, and that lack of options can be limiting, driving up rents and making it harder to live.  
  
 [Two-thirds of Ward 1 residents are renters](https://censusreporter.org/profiles/61000US11001-ward-1-dc/), making DC’s tenant protections core to our identity: they help keep long term residents in their homes and help keep the soul of the city alive. We serve as a model nationwide, and other cities look to DC as an example of what is possible. But recently these rights have been under threat as wealthy lobbyists and speculative developers strip away these rights, jack up apartment rents, and flip once-affordable single-family homes.   
  
Aparna knows that housing is a human right. As a renter and a tenant organizer, she’s seen her neighbors contend with slum conditions, face eviction, and choose between paying rent and buying necessities like food and medicine.   
  
Which is why, as the Ward 1 Councilmember, Aparna will fight for housing that prioritizes meeting people’s needs instead of trying to squeeze them for profit.  
  
### Stop Major Rent Hikes:
Bringing down rents will be Aparna’s top priority, which is why she will fight to expand DC’s rent stabilization protections. [Our rent stabilization laws date to the early 1970s](https://www.dcpolicycenter.org/publications/history-rent-control-district-columbia/), and the Rental Housing Act of 1985 still serves as the basis for today’s laws and protections. While changes have been made to rent stabilization over the years, [the law still only applies to a dwindling number of properties](https://dhcd.dc.gov/rentcontrol), namely those built before 1975, and only when the owner has five or more units. That means as older buildings come down and newer, larger buildings go up in their place, DC loses rent stabilized units.   
  
Aparna will fight to change that by pushing for an expansion of the properties subject to rent stabilization. No resident should have a steep, unpredictable rent hike just because their building was constructed in the last two decades – which is why Aparna will push for rent stabilization for all renters in multifamily buildings when the DC Council reauthorizes rent stabilization in 2030.   
  
Aparna will also fight to defend and expand renter protections. She will work to reverse some of the most [harmful provisions recently passed in the RENTAL Act](https://www.legalaiddc.org/blogs/DC-federal-RENTAL-Act) so tenants receive proper notice when they are threatened with the loss of their home, protect funding for tenant attorneys through the Access to Justice program, and strengthen anti-discrimination enforcement so that no one is denied housing for biased or unjust reasons.  
### More Housing in More Places
Critical to bringing down rents is increasing the supply of housing in Ward 1, and doing this starts with land use reform to make it easier to build more housing in more places. As Councilmember, Aparna will fight to make it legal to build apartments everywhere in DC, as has been done in places like [Portland, OR](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/03/18/portland-housing-infill-ratio-duplexes/) and [Cambridge, MA](https://www.cambridgema.gov/News/detail?path=%2Fsitecore%2Fcontent%2Fhome%2Fcdd%2Fnews%2F2025%2F2%2Fzoningformultifamilyhousingcitywide), and specifically work to upzone transit corridors such as Georgia Ave and 14th St, so high-density multifamily buildings can be constructed so we have more high-density, mixed-use buildings within a 20 minute walk of our Metro stations and within 15 minutes of bus stops. When evaluating the [update to DC’s Comprehensive Plan](https://dc2050.dc.gov/) next year, Aparna will also work to amend the [Future Land Use Map](https://dcgis.maps.arcgis.com/apps/instant/atlas/index.html?appid=353d918cca404f0aae2b171af438f434&webmap=cf9bca0a70b5488ab20a141e4d9c37b1) to ensure that DC does not entrench the same exclusionary zoning that has been law for decades. She will work to allow for [“light-touch density”](https://www.aei.org/research-products/report/legalizing-single-family-home-conversions-to-duplexes-triplexes-or-townhomes-in-washington-dcs-new-comprehensive-plan/) by reducing minimum lot sizes and setback requirements while working to expand lot coverage allowances. And in line with her transit platform, Aparna will seek to reduce or eliminate parking minimums to help encourage more use of mass transit by our residents while increasing density near transit hubs. We need to build more housing if we ever want to meet the growing need of the District – and Aparna will fight to make this building possible.   
  
Aparna will also work to modernize our permitting process, demanding faster and more efficient processes from the Department of Buildings through vigorous oversight, modernized technology, and proper staffing. She is supportive of recent legislation to ensure [buildings with 6 or fewer floors are not overly regulated](https://dc.urbanturf.com/articles/blog/dc_council_passes_one_front_door_act_opening_the_door_to_taller_single-stai/24478) and can legally be served by a single stairway. She will work to bring more types of housing to more neighborhoods, including duplexes, triplexes, and sixplexes, and make it [easier to create alley homes](https://dc.urbanturf.com/articles/blog/the_decision_that_could_lead_to_more_alley_homes_in_dc_may_be_coming_next_w/24392) by aligning their height and unit limits with their zones.
### Expand Our Tools
Aparna knows that building housing, on its own, will not be enough to meet the true needs of District residents. To do that, we need a government that will build housing of all sizes, for people at all income levels, in every neighborhood of DC. For years, the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD), the DC Housing Finance Agency (DCHFA), and the District of Columbia Housing Authority (DCHA) have failed in their respective duties to meet the needs of the people of DC. We are still [more than 37,000 deeply affordable units short of the current need](https://nlihc.org/housing-needs-by-state/district-columbia) — and without changes to how we invest in and support affordable housing development, more of our residents, especially our long-term Black and Brown residents, will be forced to leave this city.   
‍_  
Restoring and Strengthening the Tenant Opportunity to Purchase Act (TOPA):_ DC’s TOPA law sets us apart from every other jurisdiction in the country and has created opportunities for tenant power and homeownership for nearly half a century. A [Council-funded study](https://thecoalitiondc.org/news/cnhed-releases-comprehensive-analysis-of-dcs-tenant-opportunity-to-purchase-act-topa/) proved that it is the most effective tool we have in preserving and producing more affordable housing. But in recent years, we have seen these rights whittled down by Council over and over again — first with the [loss of single-family TOPA](https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/local/dc-council-votes-to-exempt-single-family-homes-from-topa-law/49347/), then [new construction downtown](https://dmped.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/dmped/page_content/attachments/Housing%20in%20Downtown_DMPED%20September%202024%20%281%29_0.pdf), and most recently with the [loss of 2-4 unit buildings](https://www.washingtonpost.com/dc-md-va/2025/10/21/rental-act-tenant-rights-dc-council/). As Councilmember, Aparna will fight to restore TOPA to every District resident and ensure that the programs that have made TOPA successful are adequately funded and properly administered, so residents can effectively exercise their TOPA rights.  
  
‍_Housing Production Trust Fund (HPTF)._ As Councilmember, Aparna will fight to invest in and expand the tools available to support the production and preservation of affordable housing that is accessible to all District residents. She will push for $125 million to be allocated to the Housing Production Trust Fund annually, and will advocate for setting production targets every five years to ensure that DC stays on track to produce affordable housing at the rate we need. And she will conduct rigorous oversight to ensure that every dollar DC invests is used to promote our shared goals of creating more deeply affordable housing. For years, there have been stories about DHCD not upholding its mission – from [making shady deals](https://www.washingtonpost.com/dc-md-va/interactive/2025/dc-bowser-housing-homeless/) [with friendly developers](https://washingtoncitypaper.com/article/771444/urban-village-apartments-duped-developers-love-bomb/), to [underutilizing critical federal programs](https://washingtoncitypaper.com/article/774255/dhcd-forfeited-35-million-federal-affordable-housing/), to [staffing issues](https://streetsensemedia.org/article/d-c-rent-control-database-launches-after-delays/) and [double dealing](https://washingtoncitypaper.com/article/779849/terrance-laney-conflict-of-interest-rental-housing-administrator-landlords/). Aparna will leverage the power of oversight and budget to ensure that DHCD leadership is accountable to the people, and that the agency performs the way District residents need it to.   
  
She will also use both oversight and legislative ability to demand public transparency and accountability for how HPTF dollars are allocated by DHCD. For years, developers have struggled to understand how projects are prioritized by DHCD because [scoring under the annual Qualified Allocation Plan has not been published](https://ggwash.org/view/92711/understanding-the-districts-housing-production-trust-fund). This leaves public financing allocation ripe for corrupt use, and makes it impossible for developers to improve their applications year over year when seeking public financing to execute affordable housing projects.   
  
She will also fight to require HPTF to support TOPA projects when reviewing applications for financing — both when tenants choose to self-purchase and when tenants assign rights to developers in exchange for a Development Agreement. Far too many units have been lost because [TOPA projects have languished](https://washingtoncitypaper.com/article/766360/topa-amazon-affordable-housing-robert-white/), waiting for permanent financing to begin rehabilitation. When projects get stuck, and developers cannot afford to make necessary repairs because they do not have permanent financing, [it is DC’s renters who suffer.](https://www.washingtonpost.com/dc-md-va/2024/09/25/dc-affordable-housing-crisis/) Aparna will fight to end this and ensure all tenants benefit from the bargaining power TOPA provides.   
  
‍_Align and Expand All Tools to Support Affordable Housing Development._ From the allocation of Low-Income Housing Tax Credits (LIHTC) to the financing provided for affordable housing through DC’s Green Bank, or programs for sustainable development administered by the DC Sustainable Energy Utility, DC has a patchwork of tools that developers tap into to build new affordable housing. But these tools are not easy to coordinate to actually get a project built. As Councilmember, Aparna will conduct oversight to align these tools and timelines so that projects do not struggle to coordinate timing to close deals and start construction. Building housing is too important to delay.   
  
‍_Social Housing._ Aparna will also work to pilot social housing in the District, a mixed-income model of public housing that will be District-owned and tenant-controlled. This pilot will be modeled on examples from just over our border in [Montgomery County, MD](https://www.hocmc.org/) or on the West Coast in [Seattle](https://www.seattletimes.com/opinion/social-housing-is-well-on-its-way-to-delivering-on-its-promises-to-seattle/) – models which prove that you do not have to go as far as Europe to find innovative and democratic solutions to the US housing crisis. DC’s Social Housing will be built by good, union jobs, meet the highest possible environmental standards, and become home to residents from all walks of life – low-income seniors and young high-income professionals alike.   
  
### End Chronic Homelessness
‍The District has failed to fully fund the social safety net for unhoused and precariously housed residents. As Councilmember, Aparna will work to end chronic homelessness by investing in programs that stabilize residents and funding the Continuum of Care so people can access both short- and long-term housing support.  
  
She will fight to fully fund the **Emergency Rental Assistance Program** (ERAP) each year and will work to reduce barriers to access, while conducting strong oversight of the Department of Human Services (DHS) to address longstanding issues like [long wait times](https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/local/dc-residents-seeking-emergency-rental-help-wait-in-line-for-hours/4019255/), [inaccessible systems](https://wamu.org/story/25/11/20/d-c-once-again-sees-technical-glitches-surging-demand-for-rent-relief-program/), and [administrative failures.‍](https://www.wusa9.com/article/news/local/dcs-emergency-rental-assistance-program-website-crashes-during-rollout/65-4ee52c19-fe82-4969-9a59-3438c922417d)Aparna will also fight for sufficient, well-maintained, and well-resourced **shelter space** by overseeing the Department of General Services and DHS to improve conditions, staffing, and services. She will work to expand shelters for people with disabilities and families of all configurations, including those with pets.  
  
Recognizing the [long, critical gap between voucher matching and lease-up](https://streetsensemedia.org/article/the-long-road-home-through-d-c-s-housing-voucher-system/), Aparna will work to **expand bridge housing programs** to provide stability during this transition while working to speed up lease-ups. She will also fight to expand voucher and subsidy programs, including ensuring adequate funding for deeply affordable housing through LRSP and Permanent Supportive Housing (PSH), increasing the number of permanent vouchers to meet need, reforming Rapid Rehousing so it no longer [abandons families in units they cannot afford](https://streetsensemedia.org/article/whats-next-for-rapid-rehousing-participant/), and expanding the [DC Flex Pilot Program](https://dhs.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/dhs/page_content/attachments/DC%20Flex%20One-Pager_2022.pdf) to support more rent-burdened households.  
  
To make homelessness rare, brief, and nonrecurring, the District must take a multi-pronged approach to bring down housing costs for working people, lower barriers to housing for folks experiencing homelessness, funding the programs that support people getting into housing, and building housing that actually meets the needs of our residents.  
### Improve Quality, Increase Accountability
For years, Aparna has organized alongside tenants across DC who are fighting to improve conditions in their homes, and knows that the District government has abandoned tenants fighting against slum conditions. As Ward 1 Councilmember, Aparna will fight to give tenants more power in that fight.   
  
For too long, the Department of Buildings has [fallen short of its mandate](https://dcist.com/story/24/01/19/dc-council-audit-housing-inspections/) to protect tenants from negligent landlords. Every year, [thousands of notices of infractions](https://dataviz1.dc.gov/t/OCTO/views/DOBPublicDashboard/ViolationsAbatementLVT?%3AshowAppBanner=false&%3Adisplay_count=n&%3AshowVizHome=n&%3Aorigin=viz_share_link&%3Aembed=yes&%3Atoolbar=no) are issued, but [only a tiny percentage are actually paid](https://www.wusa9.com/article/news/local/housing/dc-petra-development-486-housing-violations/65-e790cc70-e2af-48bf-b9c5-ed4138cba453). Additionally, DOB [rarely pursues repeat offenders through escalated legal action](https://washingtoncitypaper.com/article/758128/dc-landlords-owe-millions-in-unpaid-housing-violations/) like property liens, no matter how much they owe. As Councilmember, Aparna will push DOB to **hold landlords accountable** for the conditions they force upon their tenants by fully funding the inspectors required to do both proactive and reactive inspections, and make sure there are **real consequences for bad actors** such as those proposed in Councilmember Janeese Lewis George’s [Housing With Integrity Amendment Act of 2025.](https://lims.dccouncil.gov/Legislation/B26-0287)   
  
Aparna will introduce legislation to make it **easier for tenants to sue neglectful landlords**. Right now, [the only option readily available to tenants](https://www.legalaiddc.org/legal-info/housing-conditions-handbook) seeking to take legal action against their landlord for housing code violations is to file a case on the Housing Conditions Calendar. The power of this court is extremely limited however – [HCC judges can only order that a landlord make repairs;](https://www.dccourts.gov/superior-court/superior-court-divisions/civil-division/housing-matters/housing-conditions-court) they cannot make determinations related to rent abatements for poor unit quality or issue money judgments that require a landlord to compensate a tenant for poor treatment. Aparna will fight to level the judicial playing field by making it possible for tenants to initiate court involvement through the **creation of a public** [**rent escrow**](https://www.networkforphl.org/resources/rent-escrow-as-a-tool-for-enforcing-tenants-rights/) to which tenants can pay rent if a property owner is not holding up their end of the lease contract.   
  
When organizing with the tenants of Marbury Plaza, Aparna saw first-hand the [**impact the Office of the Attorney General**](https://oag.dc.gov/release/attorney-general-schwalb-secures-41-million) can have on the lives of everyday Washingtonians fighting against slum conditions, and as Councilmember, Aparna will work to foster a close relationship with OAG to ensure that tenants across Ward 1 and the District are served by this vital public office. She will work closely with OAG to provide public education on tenants’ rights, connect tenants struggling with bad conditions to OAG for information about what intervention OAG can make, and work with OAG to identify properties in Ward 1 where their support can make the biggest impact on the quality of life of our residents.   
  
Aparna also knows that for too long, our public housing residents have been abandoned by the District of Columbia Housing Authority and government while conditions continue to deteriorate. [DCHA is one of the biggest slumlords in the city](https://oag.dc.gov/sites/default/files/2022-10/DCReview_Final%209302022%20%281%29.pdf) and has been [plagued by scandal](https://washingtoncitypaper.com/article/777193/dcha-internal-auditor-administrative-leave-investigation-contracting-financial-mismanagemen/) and [sudden leadership turnover](https://www.wusa9.com/article/news/local/housing/dc-housing-authority-executive-director-keith-pettigrew-leaving-for-chicago-councilmember-robert-white-frustrated/65-2b926b8f-1bab-48af-bd77-af656d6f7eb9). As Councilmember, Aparna will work to make sure the District confirms Commissioners for the DCHA Board that know their job is to protect our low-income residents, [not rubber stamp bad program administration](https://hunewsservice.com/housing/d-c-residents-and-commissioners-criticize-dcha-for-inconsistent-communication/). Aparna will also fight to ensure that residents are protected when [public housing is redeveloped through programs like the Rental Assistance Demonstration Program](https://www.urban.org/urban-wire/concerns-over-privatization-dcs-public-housing-underline-larger-problems) by working to protect their rights as public housing residents through the revitalization process and beyond.   
### Promote Ownership of All Kinds
‍For too many, homeownership is out of reach. The cost of a home is too expensive, interest rates are too high, and the programs that have supported generations of Washingtonians in becoming homeowners have been [hampered and](https://ggwash.org/view/66347/100k-or-no-way-dcs-home-purchase-assistance-isnt-accessible-to-those-who-need-it) [divested from](https://dcist.com/story/24/01/11/dc-runs-out-of-homebuyer-funds-hpap/) [for too long](https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/local/dc-homebuying-assistance-program-ran-out-of-money/3375858/). As Ward 1 Councilmember, Aparna will work to make it easier for more families to achieve the dream of homeownership.
  
First, Aparna will fight to **restore the Tenant Opportunity to Purchase Act** to be a universal right. TOPA has helped thousands of households [become homeowners](https://washingtoncitypaper.com/article/174473/through-limited-equity-coops-residents-of-modest-means-can-own-property-and-prevent-displacement/) since its establishment in 1980. From the success of single-family TOPA purchase to the establishment of collectively owned limited equity cooperatives, Aparna knows that restoring TOPA to all tenants is critical to helping more families own the places they live.   
  
However, TOPA only works as well as the programs that support it. That is why Aparna will fight to **restore and reinvigorate the** [**First Right Purchase Program**](https://dhcd.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/dhcd/service_content/attachments/TOPA%20Program%20Fact%20Sheet%20final%204%2024%2017.pdf), to ensure [tenant associations exercising their TOPA rights](https://dcfpi.org/all/dcs-tools-to-create-and-preserve-affordable-housing/) have a real pathway to collective ownership and management of a building. Limited equity cooperatives are a [model of what social housing can and should look like](https://nonprofitquarterly.org/limited-equity-housing-cooperatives-why-theyre-a-solution-for-our-times/) – collectively owned and managed, deeply affordable, and places where communities look out for one another as people age.   
  
Additionally, Aparna will fight to **improve the Home Purchase Assistance Program** so that more first-time homebuyers receive the support they need to take that next step. For the last few years, DHCD has come under fire for poor administration of HPAP – from the f[ailed lottery system](https://51st.news/dc-hpap-lottery-hot-mess/) that left applicants confused to stories of [$1 million in HPAP left unspent by DHCD](https://washingtoncitypaper.com/article/774546/dhcd-fail-spend-1-million-hpap-homeowner-housing/). HPAP can also be expanded to support a greater variety of projects, such as TOPA acquisition projects in which residents would pool HPAP to support tenant purchase.   
  
Finally, Aparna will work to ensure that DHCD’s [**Small Buildings Program**](https://dhcd.dc.gov/page/small-buildings-program) **and** [**Single-Family Residential Rehabilitation Program**](https://dhcd.dc.gov/SFRRP) are fully funded and well-administered. Far too many seniors and families struggle with upkeep of single-family and small, multi-family residences alike – and no one should lose their home because they struggle to afford regular maintenance, be it an elderly single-family homeowner or an individual landlord who owns a duplex and operates it on incredibly thin margins. These programs provide critical lifelines to lower-income homeowners, but have [struggled to deliver support](https://publicintegrity.org/housing/dc-housing-program-priority-repairs-take-years/) in a timely way. The Small Buildings and Single-Family Rehabilitation programs are critical to stabilizing our seniors and families, and supporting them in maintaining homes they purchased years ago.

Everyone deserves to feel safe in DC. To do that, we need to do better than responding to crime, violence, and disorder after it’s already happened. We need to prevent violence and crime in the first place.   
  
As Ward 1 Councilmember, Aparna will fight for a District that keeps us safe: great schools and good-paying jobs; stable, affordable housing that keeps people off the streets; and free childcare that’s proven to reduce crime in the long run. By creating economic opportunity, investing in education, and building well-resourced neighborhoods, we’ll make our communities safer for everyone.  
  
‍**While working towards those goals, Aparna will also work to improve public safety, prevent crime, and improve our quality of life.** In recent years, the District has also slashed the already-too-low budgets for programs that reduce gun violence or that expand access to mental health and substance abuse treatment. Aparna will fight to reduce those cuts. She’ll fight to fix outrageous and life-threatening delays in DC’s 911 system and to protect immigrant residents from being kidnapped by masked federal agents on our streets. And she’ll fight to make sure that everyone feels safe in DC.  
### End Life-Threatening Delays in DC’s 911 System
DC’s 911 system is failing us. Callers are [put on hold during emergencies](https://www.washingtonpost.com/dc-md-va/2023/10/07/911-dc-failure-harm/), firefighters and ambulances have been [sent to the wrong addresses](https://wjla.com/news/local/dc-firefighters-wrong-address-ouc-dispatch-errors-tingey-street-statter-emergency-call-wisconsin-avenue-tingey-5th-street-mistake-wrong-location-washington-councilmember), and the District's system has “[long response times that fall short of national standards](https://washingtonian.com/2024/08/08/emergency-disservice-inside-dcs-dysfunctional-911-system/).” For someone having a heart attack or a stroke, delays could make the difference between life or death. And tragically, too-common system outages have [already cost lives](https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/local/software-update-caused-dc-911-outage-city-blames-contractor/3686022/) in DC.  
  
Aparna will fight to invest in a 911 system that delivers real public safety. DC’s Office of Unified Communications (OUC), which runs the 911 system, is under-resourced and undderstaffed—leaving calls to go unanswered or leading to dispatchers to make mistakes while working long overtime hours. She’ll fight to raise dispatcher salaries and to invest in up-to-date technology that can strengthen responsiveness and reliability.   
  
Aparna will also work to strengthen our 911 system’s emergency triage capacity. From 2019 to 2020, DC implemented a pilot paramedic call taker program that significantly [improved](https://washingtonian.com/2024/08/08/emergency-disservice-inside-dcs-dysfunctional-911-system/) emergency triage by routing certain emergency calls directly to Fire and EMS professionals. The program reduced the number of unnecessary ambulance dispatches and increased the number of cases that were effectively managed through a nurse triage line or referral to community clinics. Aparna will work to invest in a lasting, city-wide paramedic call taker program, and advocate for a pilot program to place qualified medical directors on staff to supervise dispatchers and strengthen the system’s triage capacity. She’ll also work to change the culture at OUC to help ensure that dispatchers send DC’s behavioral health professionals to respond to mental health emergencies, rather than overtaxed firefighters and EMS workers. 
### Prevent Crime and Violence

**Expanding Evidence-Based Gun Violence Prevention**: DC and other cities have successfully used a data-driven, evidence-backed, and public-health driven approach [to prevent gun violence before it happens](https://cvg.org/impact/). Violence interrupters, using the Cure Violence model, are trained, credible messengers who are tasked with peacefully resolving conflicts before they result in violence and preventing retaliation. Violence interrupters and outreach workers are tasked with identifying people at a high risk of being victims or perpetrators of violence, peacefully mediating conflicts, creating individualized risk reduction plans, and helping connect people at risk of involvement with violence with counseling and other services. Violence interrupters are typically people from and highly-respected within the community where they do this important work. Cure Violence programs have a [proven track record of success](https://cvg.org/impact/) across eight cities and recently [contributed significantly to](https://publichealth.jhu.edu/2023/new-report-finds-that-baltimores-community-violence-intervention-program-safe-streets-reduced-gun-violence) a [dramatic reduction in homicides](https://mayor.baltimorecity.gov/news/press-releases/2026-01-01-joint-statement-historic-public-safety-progress-2025#:~:text=Baltimore%20had%20133%20homicides*%20this%20year%2D%20the,58.69%25%20and%20nonfatal%20shootings%20are%20down%2057.33%25.) and gun violence in Baltimore.  
  
Aparna will fight to invest in and strengthen gun violence prevention in DC, including [the “Cure the Streets” pilot program](https://oag.dc.gov/where-and-how-cure-streets-works) and the city’s hospital-based violence interruption program. Because the Cure the Streets program is still in a pilot phase, data on its effects in DC remains limited, but [early results indicated that](https://oag.dc.gov/cure-streets-data-dashboard), once the COVID-19 pandemic ended, the program is working to reduce gun violence Ward 1 and Ward 4 hot spots. But the program—as well as the Office of Neighborhood Safety and Engagement, where it now lives—has faced constant threats of cuts from Mayor Bowser and the DC Council. This year, the Council cut the program’s budget by more than $8 million, just as it was yielding results. Aparna will fight for continued investment in violence-prevention programs, work to expand the program to more Ward 1 neighborhoods where it’s needed, and she’ll demand that the next Mayor prioritizes evidence-based violence prevention strategies as part of a comprehensive public safety strategy for every neighborhood.  
  
‍**Expanding mental health and addiction treatment**: DC needs to provide real solutions for people struggling with mental illness or substance abuse. Aparna will fight to expand DC’s [Certified Community Behavioral Health Clinic](https://www.thenationalcouncil.org/news/14-states-awarded-grants-implement-ccbhc/) program, which helps connect people who are suffering on our streets or in our communities to resources that can prevent a mental health crisis or help them break an addiction. She will fight to expand behavioral health services in schools and fund mobile health care crisis units. And she’ll fight to expand access to high-quality health insurance, including by fighting for an expanded Basic Health Plan option—which would allow all residents to buy in to a public insurance program if they chose to—and to restore funding for an expanded DC Alliance Program, including coverage for mental health and substance abuse. We are all safer when everyone in our community can get the mental health care they need.  
  
‍**Reducing Homelessness**: Communities are safer when everyone has a stable home. The District can help reduce homelessness by guaranteeing stable housing and mental health treatment—but Mayor Bower has chosen criminalizing homeless people. Aparna will fight to prevent homelessness, including by fully funding eviction protection programs [like the Emergency Rental Assistance Program](https://www.washingtonpost.com/dc-md-va/2025/11/21/emergency-rental-aid-dc-erap-eviction/). She’ll work to expand outreach staff and wraparound services for homeless people, ensuring that people receive stable housing, sustained mental health treatment, and safe transitional housing. Ultimately, research shows that [the best way reduce homelessness](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8513528/) is to provide people in crisis with stable homes, so they can address mental health and other needs. Aparna will fight to expand housing vouchers to move people out of chronic homelessnes—including by filling in for federal funding cuts that leave hundreds of DC residents at risk of losing [federal emergency housing vouchers in 2026](https://streetsensemedia.org/article/dc-housing-voucher-cuts-2026/)—and to rapidly build affordable housing for all.  

‍**Invest in engaging youth programming:** Aparna will fight to keep DC’s young people in school, where they’re safest. That starts by [investing in good schools for all](https://www.aparnafordc.com/platform), including counselors, social workers, nurses, and other support staff, so that District schools have the resources to keep students engaged in their education and so that students have adults they feel comfortable talking to. Aparna will also fight to fully fund after-school and weekend programs, and revitalize the Marion Barry Summer Youth Employment Program, which sets kids up for long-term success. Aparna will work to expand third spaces for our youth, like expanding rec center hours and providing programming that kids want, so that kids have spaces to be kids. Aparna is also running to make [childcare free for every child in DC](https://www.aparnafordc.com/platform), which will prevent crime in the long-run—early childhood education is proven to reduce adolescent crime by helping kids stay in school and develop healthy socio-emotional skills.
### Expand Our Emergency Response System
Every day, 911 receives thousands of calls— many of them for non-violent concerns like mental health crises, wellness checks, substance use issues, or noise complaints. Right now, police officers are sent to deal with all these different concerns. People dealing with an acute crisis should be seen by a professional that can support them in that moment, not a police officer. And we don’t need police officers to respond to minor concerns like wellness checks. Several cities, including [Denver](https://www.urban.org/research/publication/evaluating-alternative-crisis-response-denvers-support-team-assisted-response) and [Albuquerque](https://www.cabq.gov/acs/transparency), are shifting to first-responder models that ensure emergencies are responded to by the people best equipped to deescalate situations and provide people with wraparound support. This means having mental health professionals respond to mental health emergencies, trained counselors respond to folks managing substance use, or social workers respond to people experiencing homelessness—providing better care to people in crisis, reducing dangerous law enforcement encounters, and promoting public safety in the process.   
  
Aparna supports expanding and overhauling DC’s crisis response programs. The District has a patchwork of programs that dispatch health professionals to mental health crises and these other situations, but they are underfunded and need reform. A comprehensive pilot was launched in June 2021, and by that November, [just 2% of 911 calls about mental health crises](https://www.npr.org/local/2021/11/15/1055789888/d-c-extends-program-to-dispatch-more-911-mental-health-calls-to-social-workers) were rerouted to social workers and other health professionals. While that program was expanded and is [still being evaluated](https://thelabprojects.dc.gov/911-behavioral-health), more can and should be done. Aparna will fight for this by: investing the resources we need to guarantee that the District consistently sends high-quality health responders to respond to mental health emergencies; consolidating community responders into one program, rather than our patchwork system; ensuring 911 operators are trained to rely on mental health responders, rather than reflexively dispatching police; working to ensure 988 and 911 interoperability, and expanding the size and raising salaries of Crisis Response Teams.

## Making Government Work for You
As a democratic socialist, Aparna believes that government should work for you. ### Rat Control
The rats are out of control. DC has one of the [fastest growing rat populations](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.ads6782) in the country. From 2014 to 2024, rat complaints [skyrocketed nearly 1,200%](https://www.fox5dc.com/news/is-dc-losing-battle-against-rats-how-city-could-copy-new-yorks-model) across the District, with [Ward 1 reporting the most rats](https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/local/heres-whats-behind-the-exploding-rat-population-in-dc-experts-say/3324899/) year after year. The surge of rats is in large part due to warmer winters and an increase in restaurant and residential trash. And it means that when we take our dogs for a walk at night or get a drink at our favorite bar patio, we are all too likely to see rats scurrying by, too close for comfort.   
  
DC residents deserve a clean city where they don’t have to worry about a rat popping out of a dumpster or burrowing in their yards. And the good news is that there are steps the City can take to stop the rats. As Ward 1 Councilmember, Aparna will take on the rats by:  

- **Making Government Effective:** Aparna’s campaign is rooted in the idea of a government that works for you, which means a DC government that can deliver on cleaning up the district and tackling the rat population. To that end, Aparna will fight to ensure that DC agencies have the resources and staff needed to control rats—and to make sure they use those resources efficiently. That starts with fighting to end frequent missed trash collection by the Department of Public Works (DPW). That also includes emptying public litter cans and dumpsters before they overflow; swift replacement of trash containers; installing more public trash bins; and holding commercial property owners accountable for violations that allow rats to fester in their dumpsters. Until DPW manages trash effectively in Ward 1, nothing will change. Further, Aparna strongly supports Councilmember Janeese Lewis George’s RAT Amendment Act, which would make sure that DPW refers active rodent activity to DC Health.
- **Upgrading Our Public Trash:** Open-top public litter cans throughout Ward 1 have become an all-you-can eat buffet for DC’s rats, especially when they are overflowing. Aparna supports replacing these receptacles with smart rat-proof trash bins that include sensors to notify DPW when it’s time to empty them. She also supports new installations across DC, particularly in high-density areas and rat hot-spots. Pest-proof bins can be solar-powered with a self-compacting mechanism, allowing it to hold significantly more trash. Pasadena, California found that the upgraded containers can [increase efficiency](https://www.govtech.com/dc/articles/solar-powered-self-compacting-trash-bins-save-city.html) by reducing pick up frequency due to the larger capacity. Such “smart bins” have been installed in cities across the country, including [Boston](https://hoodline.com/2025/08/city-unveils-solar-powered-bigbelly-bins-to-tackle-litter-and-rodent-issues/), [Fairfax](https://president.gmu.edu/news/2024-02/dont-trash-it-compost-it-mason-facilities-adds-23-bigbelly-zero-waste-stations), and [Baltimore](https://www.govtech.com/fs/automation/baltimore-rolls-out-smart-trash-cans.html). Similar bins have already made their way to DC, and you can find them at the [Wharf](https://wjla.com/news/local/dc-anti-rats-trash-cans-bigbelly-recycling-bins-rat-rodents-wharf-george-washington-university-gw-prevention-pest-control-problem-restaurants-food-homes-inspectors-eat-through-holes-pmsi-infested) and on [George Washington University’s campus](https://gwhatchet.com/2024/01/21/gw-installs-outdoor-anti-rat-trash-recycling-bins/#). 
- **Rat-Proofing Household Trash Bins:** Rats love DC’s plastic household trash bins: rodents can easily get food out of their unlocked tops. Aparna will push DPW to pest-proof our trash bins, including by upgrading to locked top bins made from material that rats can’t chew through.
- **Innovating New Strategies to Stop Rat Population Growth:** The DC Rat Czar would also be empowered to test new and innovative strategies for reducing rodents, such as rat birth control which is being piloted in [Chicago](https://www.cbsnews.com/chicago/news/rat-birth-control-program-chicago-north-side/) and [New York City](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/rat-birth-control-nyc/). I’ll also seek more transparency about DPW’s 2019 rat birth control pilot conducted in Adams Morgan.
- **Expanding Composting:** The District has expanded our municipal composting pilot to 12,000 households this year, helping limit the spread of rats in our community. Aparna supports building on that success and expanding the program further, including to restaurants.
- **Appointing a DC Rat Czar:** Rats and trash go hand in hand. But rodent abatement and sanitation are managed by two separate DC agencies: the DC Department of Health (DC Health) and DPW, respectively. On the Council, Aparna will push for the creation of a DC Rat Czar who will be tasked with leading a multi-agency rat response and coordinating the work of DPW, DC Health, and other agencies to control the rat population and address the conditions that have allowed it to grow. The Rat Czar would also be responsible for helping community institutions, like Mt. Pleasant Library, secure funding and support in addressing rat burrows. The DC Rat Czar will be responsible for providing recommendations to the DC Council on policy changes to improve sanitation practices and crack down on rat infestations, and address the underlying conditions which allow them to fester.
- **Strengthening Accountability:** Aparna will vote for Councilmember Janeese Lewis George’s RAT Amendment Act, which would strengthen tools to hold negligent property owners accountable for chronic problems.

Aparna will use her oversight powers to ensure that these agreements are upheld and that the billionaires benefitting from public dollars make good on their commitments to the City. Aparna will also ensure that the $20 million annual transportation fund included in the deal goes towards public transit and supports union jobs.
- **Fight Exploitation of Immigrant Workers:** Low-road employers use the fear of immigration enforcement to silence and exploit their immigrant workers and guestworkers who organize a union, demand to be paid the wages that they’re owed, or speak up against sexual harassment. Aparna will make sure that immigrant workers are equally protected by DC law. She’s also [fighting to: end the occupation of DC](https://aparnafordc.com/platform); to end the Metro Police Department’s collaboration with ICE; and to protect delivery drivers, restaurant workers, and every immigrant who is just trying to make a living in DC from Trump’s mass deportation plans.
## Fast, Affordable Transit
DC deserves a world-class public transit system. We all need to get to school and work on time, to easily see our friends, and have the freedom to explore the city we love. And we should be able to do those things without breaking the bank and without long waits for buses that never come. In Ward 1, [taking the train or the bus](https://ggwash.org/view/99532/dc-transportation-time-tax) to get somewhere takes, on average, 2.4 times longer than driving. DC’s past promises to eliminate traffic deaths have been a [complete failure](https://www.washingtonpost.com/dc-md-va/2024/08/23/vision-zero-dc-traffic-deaths/), and Capital Bikeshare’s recent rate hike dramatically [slowed growth](https://ggwash.org/view/100873/bikeshare-beat-price-hikes-federal-crackdown-slow-cabi-ridership). Those failures hurt working families most: if you can’t afford to buy a car [with prices at record highs](https://www.cnn.com/2025/10/14/business/new-cars-top-fifty-thousand-average), you’re stuck paying a daily tax on your time.  
  
Aparna knows this feeling because she walks, takes the bus, and rides the train, just like us. And just like us, she wants D.C.’s transportation system to be faster, easier, safer, and more affordable for all.  
  
### Fast and Frequent Transit
‍Transit keeps the DMV running. Ward 1 has some of the [highest ridership bus routes](https://www.wmata.com/initiatives/ridership-portal/Metrobus-Ridership-Summary.cfm) in the entire D.C. region, but riders still face long waits, frequent delays, bus stops without shelters or seatings, or time-consuming traffic jams. And Metro is running fewer trains through Ward 1 stations than the system was designed for. We should invest in transit within the city to save us all precious time for families, for work, or just to rest. As Ward 1 Councilmember, Aparna will fight for **fast, frequent, and reliable public transit,** including with:  

- **Faster, Reliable Buses:** Aparna will push DDOT to rapidly build more bus lanes, so our buses run faster and hit fewer traffic delays. Bus lanes can [save DC workers 15% of their commuting time](https://www.wmata.com/initiatives/strategic-plans/Bus-Lanes/index.cfm). She will also support other changes to our streets that will make our buses faster, including by supporting the maximum level of bus prioritization in DDOT’s active [bus priority projects](https://buspriority.ddot.dc.gov/). She will also fight to extend the hours of all bus lanes that do not currently run 24 hours a day, such as the 16th St bus line. 
- **Increased Bus Frequency:** Aparna will also fight for increased bus service on WMATA bus routes that run entirely within DC—so that buses arrive at least every 12 minutes, all working towards the ultimate goal of having all of DC’s busiest bus routes run every five minutes. To do that, she will support funding [WMATA’s Revised Visionary Network](https://www.wmata.com/initiatives/plans/Better-Bus/upload/DC_RevisedVisionary.pdf), which would significantly increase service on many existing routes and create new routes that make it easier for Ward 1 residents to travel all over DC.
- **Congestion Pricing:** In New York, [congestion pricing has raised significant revenue for public transit](https://www.smartcitiesdive.com/news/new-york-congestion-pricing-6-month-results/752256/), all while improving traffic safety and reducing traffic. DDOT has already conducted a congestion pricing study for DC—but the Mayor won’t release it. Aparna will push the Mayor to release DDOT’s congestion pricing study. And she is very open to supporting congestion pricing if the results show the policy would benefit working families in the District.
- **More Bus Stop Shelters and Seating:** Today, the City has built bus shelters [for just about 25% of the city’s bus stops](https://ggwash.org/view/93844/how-can-dcs-bus-shelters-better-serve-riders-who-need-them-the-most). As the city has sent out a new solicitation to companies that build bus shelters, Aparna will fight to allow D.C. to install as many shelters as needed at all of D.C.'s bus stops and take in revenue from advertising on them. And in budget negotiations, she will fight to double the number of bus shelters across the city in her first term.
- **Organizing to Fund WMATA:** She’ll also organize to build support across the whole DMV for the sustainable, region-wide funding that we deserve, including via a land value tax. As she fights to expand transit, she will work towards the north star of allowing WMATA to run trains every four minutes, run buses on the region’s busiest routes every five minutes, and building new Metro lines to underserved parts of the city like Adams Morgan and Park View. 
- ‍**Better Transit for People with Disabilities:** Our transportation network must be accessible to our neighbors with disabilities. [Today, too many Ward 1 residents who rely on MetroAccess wait far too long for unreliable service](https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/local/transportation/metroaccess-sees-decline-in-customer-satisfaction/3796788/). This has happened as WMATA has privatized much of the service, handing work off to shady contractors or gig-economy companies. Privatizing service for neighbors with mobility issues is unacceptable and Aparna will pressure WMATA to bring service back in-house.

### Safer Streets
‍[Traffic deaths in DC have nearly doubled since 2014](https://ggwash.org/view/96701/breakfast-links-dc-traffic-deaths-have-doubled-concentrated-in-wards-7-and-8). That’s unacceptable. Aparna will fight for safer streets, including:  

- **Making It Easier to Install Speed Bumps and Speed Tables:** Aparna will work with the DC Department of Transportation (DDOT) to make it faster and easier to install projects that will slow down traffic to improve public safety. Aparna will also work with ANCs in Ward 1 to more quickly green-light small localized pilots that meet DDOT’s requirements.
- **A Complete, Connected Bike Network:** While DDOT’s [MoveDC plan](https://movedc-dcgis.hub.arcgis.com/pages/984de0dd663b4ce5a3d48d77d9fb3b54) has created multiple protected bike lanes in Ward 1, it is still impossible to go east to west in Ward 1 on entirely protected bike lanes. Aparna will advocate to DDOT to close these remaining connectivity gaps and make sure that you can safely ride a bike around Ward 1 and the rest of DC. 
- **Income-Based Fines for Traffic Enforcement:** When a wealthy person runs a red light, they get the same fine as a teacher, a janitor, or a student, which are nothing to ultrawealthy residents, but can be financially crushing for working-class families. Aparna supports a pilot for [“day fines,” a system in which traffic penalties are scaled to a defendant’s income](https://www.nytimes.com/2018/03/15/opinion/flat-fines-wealthy-poor.html). Day fines, [which have been used in Scandinavia for decades](https://www.ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/day-fines-european-perspective), can effectively deter speeders, and they allow the city to enforce traffic laws without bankrupting low income drivers.

### Affordable Transportation
Excellent public transportation shouldn’t break the bank. As Ward 1 Councilmember, Aparna will take the important steps to make transportation affordable for all:  

- **Keep Capital Bikeshare Affordable:** Pricing increased across the board this year, with annual membership increasing from $95 to $120 and per minute fares increasing by 50%. Aparna will work with DDOT and Capital Bikeshare to ensure that network operation expansion costs are not passed down to riders.
- **Fare Cap WMATA:** The maximum fare for a trip on Metro has been raised to $6.25 per trip. Aparna will push WMATA to follow transit agencies in New York, LA, and Denver and institute a weekly fare cap, which limits the total amount charged to any rider in a week.
- **Pilot Fare-Free Buses:** Transit should be a public good. Aparna’s term one priority is expanding frequent, reliable transit. She also supports a pilot of fare free buses as part of moving towards a free bus system in the long run.

### Good, Union Jobs
Today, DC is also [suffering from a shortage of transit workers](https://ggwash.org/view/86089/four-things-impacting-the-bus-operator-shortage-in-the-us), especially bus drivers—[driven by unacceptably low pay](https://www.epi.org/blog/the-school-bus-driver-shortage-remains-severe-and-bus-driver-pay-is-getting-worse/), high turnover, and unfair working conditions. This causes more [delays, contributes to long wait times](https://www.wusa9.com/article/news/local/dc/metry-bus-driver-shortage-route-delays/65-3b47fefb-a065-4a5a-9471-6d45864c388b), and risks limiting the growth of our bus network. Aparna is committed to raising wages and guaranteeing good, union jobs for the transit workers who make our city run.  

- **Raising the Bar for Transit Workers:** Aparna will support legislation to protect transit workers’ job security, will oppose reckless automation plans that could undermine safety, and she’ll have transit workers’ backs when they’re fighting for strong contracts.
- **Allow a Fair Transition for Transit Workers:** When the DC Council eliminated the DC Circulator, they created a relief fund for former workers. But Circulator workers still have not received those funds. Aparna will fight to ensure that Circulator workers receive the $1.6 million they deserve. Similarly, with the phaseout of the DC Streetcar, Aparna will work to ensure that Streetcar workers have a fair transition into WMATA with succession rights.
- **Enforcing Transit Agreements at the RFK Stadium:** As part of the billion-dollar deal on the RFK Stadium, the Council voted to guarantee a $20 million annual transportation fund. Aparna will fight to make sure that the transportation funding in the deal goes towards public transit and supports union jobs.
## Good Schools for All
Aparna and her brother are the proud product of public schools. Her strong public school education set her up for scholarships and financial aid that made college possible, which in turn helped her find her first job in DC. Aparna would not be in DC today if not for her education and she understands the world of opportunity that a good-quality education can open up.  
  
Parents, teachers, and students all know that public education is both a key part of what our city government does and a fundamental building block of safe and healthy communities for all. Fully funded public schools allow students to reach their potential, contribute to their community as adults and support a democratic society. For kids in high poverty neighborhoods, the impact of having a fully funded school is even greater.  
  
Providing children with a strong education, safe places to have fun and build community, and with summer jobs are vital to provide a foundation for the rest of their lives. We need to ensure that teachers are well paid and have supportive work environments, that schools are well maintained and resourced, and that students have the support they need to succeed — in and outside of the classroom.  
  
### As the Ward 1 Councilmember, here’s how Aparna will fight to improve our schools:

- **Fight For Checks And Balances In School Governance:** From underinvesting in individual schools to leaving our teachers without a contract for years, we’ve seen that mayoral control isn’t working. Mayoral control has failed to prioritize investment in our schools or create meaningful accountability, and there is not good evidence around its effectiveness, in part because of the lack of transparency around it. Instead, we should have a system in which our elected state board of education do the job the voters have chosen for them, so that we can have DCPS leadership that is responsive to the needs of our communities. This is particularly important because of the way centralized takeovers of school systems tend to happen in Black and brown districts like ours: these takeovers rob these communities of the ability to place checks on the Mayor through SBOE and Council elections. We have the opportunity to end that system by restoring school board control of schools.
- **Fully Fund Our Ward 1 Schools:** In DC, education funding as a share of the district's economic capacity is low: [DC only spends 2.2% of its gross state product on education, while the U.S. average is 3.4%](https://www.schoolfinancedata.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/profiles25_DC.pdf). DC public schools are not given the resources they need to adequately serve their community and support students’ academic achievement. Additionally, DC public schools are dealing with funding cuts. Many schools got an infusion of funding from COVID relief programs, but that funding expired last year, leaving public schools to chart an uncertain future. When schools don’t have enough funding to adequately support their students, education suffers: teachers don’t have enough supplies to teach in the classroom, students don’t have access to mental health services and schools don’t have enough paraprofessionals to support all students in the classroom. In particular, Harriet Tubman Elementary has been facing budget cuts at the same time they are in the process of relocating, which is destabilizing to students and families alike. This needs to end — no parent should worry that their child’s education is in a state of precarity.
- **Ensure Schools Have The Resources They Need:** We’ve seen schools continuously report a lack of maintenance - heat broken in the winter, ceilings that are crumbling, broken elevators - that risk school safety and affect students’ ability to learn. Aparna plans to use her oversight power over DCPS facilities to ensure that Ward 1 schools have what they need to function - safe water and air, air conditioning and heat, working locks, elevators, and appliances, and more. 
- **Improve Teacher Retention with Stronger Benefits:** Ensuring that teachers are compensated appropriately for their credentials and skills is key to attracting and retaining high quality teachers. Unfortunately, [DC public school teachers earn 19.5% less than similarly qualified workers in the city](https://www.epi.org/publication/the-teacher-pay-penalty-reached-a-record-high-in-2024-three-decades-of-leaving-public-school-teachers-behind/), making recruiting and retaining teachers difficult. Raising teacher pay ensures that teachers and staff can work sustainably to provide quality instruction and support to all students in the classroom. Aparna also supports expanding other benefits that will improve retention, including providing teachers more flexibility and more planning time, and extending policies to support international educators in DC.
- **Strengthen Teacher Voice:** DC’s educators know what our kids need to learn and thrive. Aparna will engage teachers to make sure their ideas help drive education reform in the district, such as including them in the budget process and ensuring schools have the resources they need. She also supports strengthening public sector workers’ right to organize and collectively bargain, including by [guaranteeing teachers the same basic labor rights as private-sector workers](https://aparnafordc.com/platform).
- **Expand Community Schools and Wrap-Around Supports:** Students often depend on their schools for health, nutrition, and as mentioned above, behavioral health needs. Community schools offer wrap-around support services to students and their families, which help them succeed academically and in life. Our neighbors in Baltimore have demonstrated this model of wrap-around support through [Community Schools is possible](https://www.baltimorecityschools.org/article/1366231), expanding the number of community schools from 50-130 schools between 2018-2022 and seeing [school violence decrease as a result](https://mddems.org/news-clips/community-violence-reduction-initiative-to-be-expanded-to-baltimore-city-schools/#:~:text=Community%20violence%20reduction%20initiative%20to,year%20to%20fund%20the%20program.). Aparna will work with DC Public Schools to expand the number of community schools in the District to provide safe spaces for all students to thrive.
- **Keep Ice Out Of Schools:** Schools need to be safe for all of our students, and that means keeping Trump’s brutal immigration policy out of these spaces. In Aparna’s Stand up for DC Platform, she commits to exercising real oversight and ending cooperation with ICE. Ward 1 schools are particularly vulnerable to this kind of abuse, and we know that ICE has already targeted them, so it’s important that our leaders commit to coordinating resources and volunteers to provide safe travel to and from school and other needs. As your Councilmember, Aparna will use her office to organize to ensure these outcomes.
- **Increase Funding For School-Based Behavioral Health:** Students can’t learn when they are dealing homelessness or unstable housing, or mental health crises. These issues, which have been exacerbated by DC’s housing affordability crisis and the COVID-19 pandemic mean that DC public schools need to increase funding in mental and behavioral health, not decrease it. Sadly, the FY 2026 budget includes a [$2.3 million reduction](https://wearedcaction.org/publications/dcs-fy26-budget-fails-youth-at-a-critical-time/) in School-Based Behavioral Health interventions, affecting both crisis services and community response teams. Instead, funding for school-based behavioral health should increase to meet the needs of students today. 
- **Expand DC After-School Programs:** Education doesn’t stop when the school bell rings and working parents often need additional care support. Young students who may have higher care needs benefit from the increased time and attention of after school programs and older students benefit by continuing their education or participating in jobs programs, which reduces their likelihood of getting involved in risky behaviors. Aparna supports expanding DC after school programs to ensure all students have access to these resources.
- **Ensure Transparency and Accountability from Charter Schools:** While charter schools receive public funding, they are not currently held to the same transparency, reporting, and labor protection standards as DCPS schools. Parents and DC residents deserve an understanding of how our education system is operating across all different school types, and schools that receive public funds should be held to the same standards. As your Councilmember, Aparna will fight to make sure this is the case. 
- **Make School Meals Free For All Students:** Students need to be fed to be able to learn, and making free school meals universal reduces stigma, administrative burden, and ambiguity. Aparna supports the Universal Free School Meals Amendment Act of 2025, which would provide school meals and after school snacks for all DC students. 
- **Fund Free Early Childhood Education:** Early childhood interventions help set children up for success by the time they go to grade school. DC has some of the highest early [childhood education costs in the country](https://www.axios.com/local/washington-dc/2025/05/28/d-c-parents-pay-most-for-daycare-in-u-s), with the typical family now paying [more than $22,000 on childcare, per kid, every year](https://www.epi.org/child-care-costs-in-the-united-states/#/DC), and spots are not guaranteed. This burden falls especially heavily on mothers and low-income families, who have to make the impossible choice between staying in the workforce, with a large chunk of their check going to child care, or leaving their paying jobs because they simply can’t afford early childhood education. Aparna plans to fight for free childcare for every child in DC, from 6 months to Pre-K. She will also fight to expand the total number of high-quality early childhood education providers in the District, so no family is scrambling to get a spot.
