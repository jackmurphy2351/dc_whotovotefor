# Meet Trent

**Trent Holbrook** is running for Congress as the only candidate with a record of leadership and success on the legislative priorities critical to D.C. residents.

For the past eight years, Trent served in legislative roles for Congresswoman Eleanor Holmes Norton, including as **Senior Legislative Counsel**, where he led progress on D.C. statehood, D.C. home rule, and issues facing federal employees.  He was the chief strategist who took the D.C. statehood bill from losing in 1993 to passing in the House of Representatives twice—securing record support and a unanimous Democratic vote the second time.

Trent's public service career extends beyond Congress.  He previously worked in the Executive Branch, including successfully working at **AmeriCorps** to defend the agency from Congressional attempts to eliminate its funding.  This comprehensive background, which includes time as a legal intern in the **Obama White House Counsel's Office**, gives him the unparalleled expertise needed to fight for D.C.'s complex federal issues.  Trent is an alumnus of the Georgetown University Law Center, Austin College, and Oxford University.
# Issues
## Civil Rights
### Voting rights (Including D.C. Statehood!)
The single most important voting rights issue in our country right now is the inability of District of Columbia residents – who pay more income taxes per capita than any state and more in overall taxes than the residents of nearly half the states – to vote for Senators and a Representative to Congress.  We cannot vote for local elected officials who can do their job without federal interference, including Congress overturning the laws and regulations that they enact and promulgate.  **My top priority in Congress will be D.C. statehood.**  I already led the successful effort to pass the bill in the House of Representatives twice, and am the only person positioned to take the first steps on day one to do so again, get it through the Senate, and eventually make D.C. the 51st state.

As District of Columbia residents, we have earned our right to be the 51st state.  District residents have fought and died in every single American war since the Revolution.  We pay the full freight of federal taxes, including that we pay more overall in federal taxes than roughly half the states and pay more personal income tax per person than the residents of any state in the country.  We abide by the same laws and rules as every other American – even though some of those laws treat us unequally.  Moreover, we are of all stripes and from all walks of life.  When Congress recently cut D.C.’s local budget by over a billion dollars, it didn’t harm wealthy lobbyists.  It hurt teachers, police officers, and the local business owners impacted by these cuts.  We had an out-of-control presidential administration attempt to federalize our police department; we have had the military marching in our streets; and we have had federal law enforcement officers – unaccountable to D.C. residents – enforcing local laws in ways that could not happen anywhere else in the country because we lack statehood.  I have fought on all of these issues.  In fact, I have been the main legislative staffer on the Hill working on these issues for the past eight years.  

Let’s be clear – the attacks on D.C. are because Republicans know that we are so tantalizingly close to becoming the 51st state.  They are afraid of us having our equal rights and voting for who we want to represent us.  Republicans have cynically argued that D.C. statehood is unconstitutional.  They are wrong.  They simply want to deny 700,000 people the right to vote because they think we will vote for Democrats.  Democracy should not depend on who you vote for.  If Republicans don’t want Democrats elected in D.C., they should campaign better and actually get our votes.  With that said, DC. statehood is constitutional.  It is constitutional under the admissions clause, the enclave clause, and the 23rd amendment to the Constitution.  Congresswoman Norton was my law professor at Georgetown before I started working with her.  I have spent the last eight years making the constitutional case for D.C. statehood.  I am fully ready to defend the constitutionality of D.C. statehood against any incredible attack Republicans will inevitably make.  I am the person most able to make that complicated legal case to Congress and to the American people.

With that said, there are other national voting rights issues that I will continue to focus on.  First, we must pass the **Freedom to Vote Act**, which will prohibit gerrymandering of congressional districts and require universal early voting and vote-by-mail.  It will modernize voter registration, including allowing for same day registration, restore voting rights to returning citizens, and provide campaign finance reform.  When it passed the House when the Democrats were in the majority, the bill, then called the For the People Act, contained language that I personally drafted and worked to get included that called for D.C. statehood.  This language that I advanced, under Congresswoman Norton’s leadership, was the first time either chamber of Congress voted in favor of D.C. statehood in American history.

Next, we must pass the **John R. Lewis Voting Rights Advancement Act**.  Named for another civil rights giant, this bill will update the Voting Rights Act to take into account the Supreme Court’s horrific decision in _Shelby County v. Holder_.  The Voting Rights Act of 1965 required certain states with a history of racial discrimination in voting to “preclear,” or get approval from the Department of Justice or a federal court, before changing their voting rules.  The need for this rule was apparent, especially when the Voting Rights Act was enacted in 1965.  The Supreme Court in _Shelby County_ held, 5-4, that the formula for federal review is outdated and therefore violates the constitution.  The Supreme Court created, from thin air, the concept of state equality, meaning you could not treat states differently – even though some had long and particular histories of discrimination.  As Justice Ginsburg opined in her magisterial dissent, changing the rules now because these states are not currently taking discriminatory actions “is like throwing away your umbrella in a rainstorm because you are not getting wet.”  She was foresighted, as always.  Almost immediately after _Shelby County_, 11 states passed laws that otherwise would have been subject to preclearance, and at least 22 states have passed laws to restrict vote by mail access.  I have worked with Congresswoman Norton to introduce a resolution every two years calling on Congress to condemn these actions of the states that limit the right to vote.  As the Supreme Court said elsewhere in 1886, and correctly, voting is “a fundamental political right because [it is] preservative of all rights.”  We must pass the John R. Lewis Voting Rights Advancement Act to restore common sense to our voting systems.

I highlight these two bills because they, with the Washington, D.C. Admission Act, the bill to make Washington, Douglass Commonwealth the 51st state, are the three most significant voting rights bills being advanced, and we must work to continue to unite the D.C. statehood bill with these other two bills that Democrats champion and will eventually pass when we have the majority.
### Abortion and Personal Bodily Autonomy
**A woman should have the right to choose.  Everybody should have the right to bodily autonomy.**  I have spent much of my time working in Congress for Congresswoman Norton fighting for this value.  In 2022, the Supreme Court overturned _Roe v. Wade_ and a half a century of precedent recognizing a woman’s right to terminate a pregnancy.  Roe did not come out of thin air.  It was based on older precedent that recognized the rights to privacy and bodily autonomy.  Moreover, the Supreme Court was only able to reverse Roe because of the seats on the Supreme Court stolen by President Trump and the Republicans.  I helped lead the fight in Congress, through the Congressional Black Caucus, to stop the confirmation of these right-wing extremist justices.  We weren’t successful on that front, but we have continued to make the case for why we need to reform our nation’s highest court.  When we have a Democratic president and Senate, we should expand the Court.

The overturning of _Roe_ is especially problematic for D.C. residents.  For the half a century _Roe_ was the law of the land, Congress could not prohibit abortion in D.C. because that would go against that long held right.  Now, Republicans can.  Even before _Roe_ was overturned, D.C. women were particularly targeted because of the longstanding rider (language inserted into another bill) that prohibits D.C. from spending its own local funds (not federal funds) on providing abortion services for low-income women.  No matter a person’s view on abortion, D.C. should not be prohibited from spending its own local funds in the way that states can.  I strongly oppose this rider and have fought for the last eight years to try to remove it.  When Democrats were in the majority in the House, it was removed from the House spending bills.  I will continue to fight to remove this anti-home rule rider once and for all.

Abortion is not the only reproductive health issue on which Congress has targeted D.C. residents.  Congressional Republicans have long targeted D.C.’s Reproductive Health Nondiscrimination Act, a local law that prohibits discrimination based on one’s reproductive history.  Again, D.C. should be able to enact its own local laws.  Fortunately, while this has also appeared as a rider in appropriations bills, it has not been enacted, and D.C.’s law has not been overturned.  I will continue to fight in Congress to protect this D.C. law, the same as I have for the last eight years, and I will fight to protect all D.C. laws, even if I disagree with them substantively.  See more on abortion under "Healthcare".
### LGBTQ Equality
**I will be a champion for LGBTQ+ equality while in Congress.**  I have served as Congresswoman Norton’s staffer on LGBTQ+ issues, including working with the House Equality Caucus, and will continue to advance full equality for all of our residents.  **I will join the Equality Caucus** as an LGBTQ+ ally when I come to Congress.

As with so many other so-called “social issues,” D.C. has a history of being targeted for protecting its LGBTQ+ residents.  Congressional Republicans have targeted many of D.C.’s equality laws.  For example, Congressional Republicans have long targeted D.C.’s Reproductive Health Nondiscrimination Act, a local law that prohibits discrimination based on one’s reproductive history.  Again, D.C. should be able to enact its own local laws.  Fortunately, while this has also appeared as a rider in spending bills, it has not been enacted, and D.C.’s law has not been overturned.  I will continue to fight in Congress to protect this D.C. law, the same as I have for the last eight years.

I will support the **Equality Act**, one of the most important modern civil rights pieces of legislation.  The Equality Act will amend the nation’s major civil rights laws to explicitly prohibit discrimination based on sexual orientation and gender identity.  Companies should not be able to discriminate based on who one loves.  This is long overdue.

Our LGBTQ+ community, and especially our trans community, is under greater attack than at any time since marriage equality was recognized by the Supreme Court in 2015 in _Obergefell v. Hodges_, one of the greatest civil rights cases in Supreme Court history, and which is under attack by the right-wing extremists on the Court.  While the country, fortunately, is experiencing a decline in overall violent crime, hate crimes, including against members of the LGBTQ+ community, continue to rise.  D.C. does not have a locally-elected or appointed prosecutor, and almost all crimes are prosecuted by the U.S. Attorney.  The U.S. Attorney is appointed by the President, and confirmed by the Senate, where we have no Senators.  If we were a state, we would have “senatorial courtesy” where our Senators could make recommendations to the President on who should be in this important position.  Though a Delegate, Congresswoman Norton was granted the equivalent of senatorial courtesy under Democratic presidents, but not Republicans.  I will again seek senatorial courtesy from every president so that we can have some say in who prosecutes crimes.  D.C. should also have a local prosecutor to prosecute our local crimes, not the U.S. Attorney, who is not responsible to D.C. residents.  I will continue this fight for a local prosecutor, which I have worked on with Congresswoman Norton, when I am in Congress.  This is important for everybody in D.C., but it is especially important for the LGBTQ+ community.

Congress has also forced a private school voucher program in the District that does not have the same civil rights protections, including for LGBTQ+ students, as public schools.  **I will continue to fight, as I have for the last eight years, to require federally-required private schools to at least adhere to D.C.’s civil rights laws.**
## Federal Employees
**Stop the Firings, Give Pay Raises, Protect Retirement**

Federal employees are under greater attack than perhaps at any moment in American history, and at least since the transition to our modern system of government under the New Deal in the 1930s.  Federal employees have been fired, harassed, had their benefits taken, and endured stagnant wages.  **I will request to serve on the House Oversight Committee** (Committee), which has jurisdiction over most federal employee issues.  I have worked closely with Congresswoman Norton for the past eight years with her work on this Committee.  This administration has acted unconstitutionally, despite what a rogue Supreme Court has said in overturning precedent after precedent.  Trump, Elon Musk and his other cronies have illegally fired federal employees by violating the due process, appropriations, appointments and other clauses of the Constitution.  The Trump administration refuses to spend money that Congress has appropriated on programs that Congress has authorized.  Congress creates the laws and has the power of the purse, not the executive, and for good reason.  This is basic constitutional law, and the Trump administration has ignored the law, and our federal employees have suffered as a result.  Having one of the most senior administration officials, the Director of the Office of Management and Budget (OMB), say, “We want the bureaucrats to be traumatically affected.  When they wake up in the morning, we want them to not want to go to work, because they are increasingly viewed as the villains.  We want their funding to be shut down.  We want to put them in trauma,” is unconscionable and ignorant.  These are the opposite of the principles for which OMB and, indeed, America, should stand.

Working for Congresswoman Norton, I **have worked for the past eight years with federal employee unions, federal employees themselves, and others to protect and expand benefits to which federal employees should be entitled.**  I worked on legislation to expand federal employee benefits to make sure every federal employee is entitled to every **across-the-board pay raise** and **cost of living adjustment** available.  I have worked to try to **provide backpay to federal contractors following federal government shutdowns**.  I have fought for **pay raises for federal employees** and to ensure that they receive the same raises or adjustments as members of the military – both groups should enjoy these benefits.  I have led efforts to **ensure that federal agencies do not leave the national capital region**, which harms not only federal employees, but the important work these federal agencies do.
## Criminal Justice
### Federal Bureau of Prisons
I have worked tirelessly on Federal Bureau of Prisons (BOP) issues over the last eight years.  Most of our residents convicted of felonies are sent to BOP to serve their sentences.  D.C. residents are often placed in BOP facilities many miles away from D.C. in far away states.  I led Congresswoman Norton’s efforts to get language included in the **First Step Act** in 2018 to **make sure D.C. residents were to be kept within 500 miles of D.C.**whenever possible.  We also worked to get included in the First Step Act language explicitly saying that **the programming and good time credits in that Act would apply to individuals in BOP custody for D.C. Code offenses**.  We fought to get this implemented by BOP for years, and in 2023 the D.C. Council passed a law making it explicit that D.C. Code violators would be eligible for these credits.  Because of our tireless efforts, BOP has finally just now recognized and started applying good time credit to those who participate in this programming.  I will continue to work to make sure D.C. residents who want to do this programming to help with their reentry receive the credits they have earned.

D.C. residents in BOP custody for D.C. or federal felonies also typically go to a federal halfway house before fully reentering society after their sentences.  Since the closure of Hope Village during the pandemic, I have **fought to get our returning citizens into a facility in D.C. instead of in Maryland**.  Most men are currently in a halfway house in Baltimore. This is not acceptable.  Reentry and reintegration are integral to reducing recidivism.  Our residents need to be in D.C.  I have fought to make sure that the next halfway house for our residents is in D.C., and I will continue to keep up this fight when I am in Congress.  While in prison, D.C. residents should also be kept close to D.C.  We have fought to have all individuals in BOP custody be located in one facility near D.C. to make it easier for families to visit, easier for reintegration into society, easier to have D.C. services provided to them, and otherwise make it more convenient for our community.  This has repeatedly been denied.  I will continue to push for this in Congress, even if it requires BOP to build a new facility.  In the meantime, D.C. residents should be allowed to serve the end of their sentences in D.C., at the D.C. Jail if necessary.  BOP has refused this as well.  I will continue to fight to make it happen, pursuing all legislative options available to me.
### Policing
There are over a dozen federal police agencies with at least some jurisdiction in the District of Columbia.  We need to ensure that these federal officers meet all of the standards we expect of modern policing.  I strongly support the **George Floyd Justice in Policing Act** and will support it in Congress.  With many other important reforms, this bill would make police accountable in court if they violate the law, improve transparency, and ban no-knock warrants and racial profiling.  It also contains the text of Congresswoman Norton’s bill, that I worked on and that I helped to get included, to **require federal officers to wear body cameras and use dashboard cameras**.  These protect everybody, including the police.  I will continue to fight for these important policing reforms in Congress.
### Federalizing MPD and the Military on Our Street
Let me say in the strongest possible terms that **I vehemently oppose the federalization of the Metropolitan Police Department** (MPD).  While this authority has in theory rested with the president through the Home Rule Act for the last half century, President Trump is the first President ever to attempt to use it.  And, of course, he did so **illegally**.  The mayor is required to provide the assistance of MPD only if there is an emergency and if that emergency is federal in nature.  When Trump attempted to federalize MPD, there was no federal emergency.  He said that it was necessary to fight crime.  This is not a federal issue, and, more importantly it was an outright lie.  Crime in D.C. is down, so there was no legal justification for this.  Moreover, if crime were an emergency, why did Trump wait nine months to do this, when crime was further dropping?  Even if Trump hadn’t lied, no president should have this authority to federalize our police department in a way he or she couldn’t even dream of doing elsewhere.  I helped to get the House Oversight Committee to pass Congresswoman Norton’s **District of Columbia Police Home Rule Act** to remove this authority from the President, and I will continue to advance similar legislation when I am in Congress and will finally get this done.

President Trump also has used the D.C. National Guard to enforce local law in D.C. in a way he could not do anywhere else in the country.  This is one more reason we need to **give control over the D.C. Guard to the mayor**, and why we ultimately need statehood.
### Federal Judges
If the District of Columbia were a state, we would have what is called “**senatorial courtesy**” to make recommendations or oppose nominees for vacancies on our federal court.  We are not guaranteed that since we are not a state and we have no senators.  Instead, the President can impose any nominee he wants to fill our court, so long as the Senate confirms them.  This is wrong.  This is anti-democratic.  Unlike judges in almost any other district in the country, judges on the D.C. district court do not even have to live in D.C.  Congresswoman Norton requested and received the equivalent of senatorial courtesy herself for D.C. federal judges from Democratic presidents.  The Republicans she requested it from refused.  I led her efforts to nominate highly qualified individuals to this important court, helping to shape the court for years to come.  I will continue this work when I am elected by again **requesting senatorial courtesy and fighting nominees otherwise forced onto us**.  I worked with Congresswoman Norton’s task force she created and assigned the responsibility of making recommendations to her to send to the president for D.C. federal court vacancies.  I will maintain a taskforce for this purpose, with representatives from each of D.C.’s wards.  I also led Congresswoman Norton’s work as the chair of the **Congressional Black Caucus’s (CBC) Judicial Nominations Taskforce** before the structure of the taskforces were changed in recent Congresses.  I helped research the jurisprudence of the most problematic of Trumps’ judicial nominees the CBC opposed during his first term.  **I will continue to work with all my colleagues to oppose problematic nominees for the District of Columbia and throughout the country.**
### Marijuana
**Adult-use and medical marijuana should be legal everywhere.**  District of Columbia residents passed a referendum legalizing adult-use marijuana in 2014.  Immediately after this, however, Congress enacted a rider, introduced by a member of Congress not from D.C., to the D.C. spending bill prohibiting D.C. from enforcing that law and having it take effect.  However, Norton discovered a loophole in the poorly drafted language.  The law already was in effect.  Therefore, marijuana was legal, but we have been left with the absurd loophole that D.C. cannot properly regulate it.  **I will work to remove this rider that should never have been enacted in the first place.**  I worked with Congresswoman Norton to get it removed from appropriations bills when Democrats were in the majority, but Republicans in the Senate refused to pass the bill without it.  (Appropriations bills require 60 votes in the Senate, so they almost always need Republican votes to pass even when Democrats are in the majority.)  Even when Republicans were in the majority in the House, the Republican chair of the subcommittee responsible for this bill removed the rider, but members of the full committee, again not from D.C., worked to get it included back into the bill.  It is long past time for D.C. to be able, just like the states, to fully regulate marijuana in its jurisdiction.  No matter one’s view on marijuana, there is no reason D.C. should be treated less than the states.
## Military and Foreign Policy
### The military, whether to go to war, and the D.C. National Guard
Whether to go to war or send resources to foreign countries fighting in wars are some of the most consequential questions a member of Congress will ever face.  Indeed, it is one of the most difficult questions society must ever confront.  However, D**istrict of Columbia residents have zero say in whether to send our residents to war** – and this despite the fact that District residents have fought and died in every single American war since the American Revolution.  This is wrong.  This is another important reason we need statehood.

Moreover, every state and every territory that has a National Guard has the authority to control that Guard through its governor.  Not so with D.C.  In D.C., the president, not the mayor, controls the D.C. National Guard.  This is wrong, and we have seen several instances where having the president given this authority, instead of the mayor, has led to disastrous results.  This includes when Trump used the Guard to clear Lafayette Square near the White House for a photo op and when he refused to call in the D.C. National Guard on January 6th when the Capitol and democracy itself were under attack.  Moreover, the president can use the National Guard to enforce local D.C. law.  While the president cannot use a state National Guard to enforce domestic law, D.C. is a possible exception to this rule.  **We must pass the District of Columbia National Guard Home Rule Act to give the mayor the authority over the National Guard in the same way the governors of states and territories with Guards have.  I worked on this bill with Congresswoman Norton and together we got it passed through the House when Democrats were in the majority.**  Enacting this bill will be a top priority when I am in Congress.

**I will also continue to support our D.C. National Guard.**  I have been the leader in Congress to try to prevent our National Guard members from having to pay back money that they have rightfully earned.  I will also work to increase federal education awards available to them.

Despite District of Columbia residents paying all federal taxes and having met all the other obligations of citizenship, including having fought and died in every single American war since the Revolution, we do not get the same number of appointments to America’s service academies as states.  I worked to get legislation passed in the House when Democrats were in the majority to **give D.C. the same number of appointments to our prestigious academies** we would receive if we were a state.  We deserve this.  I will introduce a similar bill again and work to get it enacted.

As we must support D.C. statehood to ensure full democracy for all Americans, on issues of national policy, America must continue to fully support democracy around the world.  Importantly, this includes providing unwavering support for Ukraine’s struggle against Russia.  Ukraine is our ally and a democracy, and we must continue to lead the world in protecting it from this unlawful invasion by President Putin.  We must stand up when China and others support autocracies over democracies.  I am pleased by the release of the Israeli hostages and am hopeful about a ceasefire in Gaza.  We must find a way to ensure that this is the last major war in the Middle East, and we must recognize Palestine as a state.

We can only do this important work by engaging in diplomacy and aid around the world.  **We must reestablish and fully fund USAID and return the U.S. State Department to where it was before the actions of the Trump Administration.**

Despite not having a vote on final passage on bills, these international and military issues will come up for the Delegate on the House floor in what is called the Committee of the Whole, which is where amendments to bills are considered on the House Floor, in committee, and as the Delegate engages in advocacy through, for example, sending letters with colleagues.  I have spent the last eight years working with Congresswoman Norton on all of these aspects of the Delegate’s role.
## The Economy and Education
### The Economy
The economy is not where it needs to be.  People are struggling to make ends meet.  Meanwhile, Republicans in Congress are cutting the social safety net while giving massive tax breaks for the wealthy.  This is repulsive.  We should increase the number of tax brackets and **increase the tax rate on the highest brackets** – substantially.  We should also eliminate the arbitrary and unfair distinction between taxes on earned income and taxes on capital gains.  **Millionaire investors should not pay roughly half the taxes they would pay had they earned their income.**  Taxing capital gains  the same as earned income will increase revenue to the federal government and can help preserve and even expand our social programs that we have had for decades.  It will also help alleviate income inequality.  **We must stand behind our unions**, including by protecting the right to organize and passing the **Protecting the Right to Organize (PRO) Act**, which would help protect worker bargaining rights.  **I will join the Labor Caucus in Congress.**

To return to a D.C. specific issue, because D.C. is not a state, D.C. sometimes is not treated like a state for the purposes of federal funding.  It should be treated as a state, a county, and a city, because it operates as all three.  **I will continue to fight to make sure D.C. receives the full funding to which it is entitled.**  For example, when Congress enacted one of its stimulus bills during the pandemic, it did not treat D.C. like a state and Congresswoman Norton and I had to work tirelessly to get D.C. the funding it deserved.  D.C. residents pay the full range of federal taxes – actually we pay more in personal income taxes per capita than any state and more overall in taxes than roughly half the states.  We deserve to be treated like a state for the purposes of federal funding – and for everything else, for that matter.  **I will also introduce legislation to treat D.C. at least as well as states, cities and counties for federal funding purposes in law that has already been enacted, and that it will similarly be treated in this same way in future legislation unless that law explicitly says otherwise.**  (If a law says that D.C. won't be treated like the states, I would oppose it.)  I will also work to  **transfer as much federal land to the District as possible**, which will spur further economic development.

Recently, Republicans forced D.C. to cut the equivalent of over two billion dollars from its own local budget simply because they could… or because it was a drafting error… or because they could.  Republicans couldn’t decide which narrative to tell.  Either way, I will work to ensure that never happens again and make the case that this is yet another reason we need D.C. statehood.
### D.C. Education Issues
D.C. has had private school vouchers forced upon it by Congress.  District residents did not want this, the private schools do not have the same record of success as other D.C. schools, and these schools do not have to adhere to the same civil rights laws as other D.C. schools.  This is wrong.  **I will work to undo this unjust system and fight for federal funding for D.C. schools without having vouchers forced upon us.**  Just like the states, we can decide for ourselves how to administer public education in our jurisdiction.

I will also fight to **preserve the D.C. Tuition Assistance Grant program, or DCTAG**.  This important program, funded at $40,000,000 per year, provides the difference between in and out of state tuition for D.C. residents attending out of state schools, up to $15,000 per year (and up to $3,750 for those attending D.C. schools of higher education).  **I worked with Congresswoman Norton to successfully increase the awards for this critically important program by 50%.**  I have fought for the last eight years to preserve and increase this funding, even while Congress (and even some D.C. officials) have sought to reduce the amount of money D.C. receives for it.  Congresswoman Norton got this program started in 1999 when she noticed many District residents were leaving D.C. when their children were getting to be of college age because of far lower in-state tuition rates at state schools elsewhere.  DCTAG has helped keep residents in D.C. while giving D.C. students much needed tuition assistance.  **I will work to preserve this program and get rid of the income caps for it.**

**I will fight for student loan forgiveness** and ensure that it is applied accurately.  Individuals who complete 120 payments toward their student loans while working for a nonprofit or government entity can have their student loans forgiven.  This is an incredibly important program, and especially at a time when federal employees are under historic attack.  I will work to preserve this program and make sure that it is implemented properly.  I will also fight to stop this administration from targeting institutions of higher education illegally.  D.C. is a “college town,” with numerous excellent universities.  We must protect them from illegal interference.  Higher education is the reason our society is as successful as it is today, and this must be preserved from right wing extremist ideology.

I am also an avid supporter of **national service**.  I spent three years working in congressional affairs at AmeriCorps before attending law school.  I was there when we worked to save AmeriCorps from being zeroed out by Congressional Republicans.  We were successful because we made the case for why this bipartisan-supported program is so important for society.  AmeriCorps is unnecessarily and unconstitutionally under attack, and I will continue to fight for it and for the education awards individual AmeriCorps members receive upon completing their service.  **I will proudly join the Congressional National Service Caucus.**
## Healthcare
### Expanding the Affordable Care Act
**In this wealthy country, healthcare should be a right.**  Every single American should have access to healthcare.  I am a strong supporter of the **Affordable Care Act (ACA)**.  The ACA led to the greatest coverage of Americans in U.S. history, and brought us to virtual universal coverage.  I will work to reverse the damage that the Trump Administration has caused to the American healthcare system and expand coverage by working for a **public option** to help further reduce the cost of healthcare.  I will work to restore the subsidies to purchasing healthcare that the Republicans removed in their Big Ugly Bill.  I will also work to **restore the minimum essential coverage standards that prohibited junk plans that harmed consumers** and that Republicans allowed after the ACA prohibited them.  I support an individual mandate to purchase health coverage because that is the only way to lower the costs of healthcare for everybody.  If only sick people purchase health insurance, costs go up.
### Protecting D.C.’s Medicaid Reimbursement Rate
I will work to **protect D.C.’s Medicaid reimbursement rate** from cuts by the federal government, which Republicans have proposed.  One of the primary reasons that D.C. receives a higher reimbursement rates is because it is not allowed to tax income earned in D.C. by residents of other states, like any other state can.  Our slightly higher Medicaid reimbursement rate from the federal government makes up only a part of the difference in the lost income D.C. would receive because of this anti-home rule prohibition.
### Protecting a Woman’s Right to Choose
**I will also work to protect a woman’s right to choose and will work to remove the rider prohibiting D.C. from spending its own local funds on abortion services for low-income women.**  I will also continue my work to defend D.C.’s Reproductive Health Nondiscrimination Act, which prohibits employers from asking about an employee’s reproductive history.  I will ensure that insurance companies must fully pay for all reproductive care needs.
