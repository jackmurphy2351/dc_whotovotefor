## Openings (abridged)

[Pinto] Ward 2 councilmember; running to ensure "every Washingtonian has the opportunity for economic prosperity," to build more affordable housing and invite new industries, to keep every neighborhood safe, and to "protect our autonomy and ensure that we are the 51st state." Frames herself as able to both protect DC and "leverage this moment into opportunities" and "secure more resources from the federal government."
[Jaczko] Physicist; 27 years in DC; staff to Rep. Markey and Sen. Reid; Obama-appointed NRC chair. Runs because the Trump administration "has launched an assault on the rule of law, on our federal workforce, and really on the district." Will fight for statehood "in a novel way" — focusing on the *taxation* half of "no taxation without representation" by **exempting DC from federal income taxes until statehood**; will work to lower electricity prices; will work to restore the federal workforce ("tens of thousands of jobs" lost).
[Holbrook] Norton's senior legislative counsel for eight years; took the statehood bill from its 2-to-1 loss in 1993 to passing the House twice (unanimous among Democrats the second time). Led the House passage of the DC National Guard Home Rule Act; fought Trump's "illegal federalization" of MPD; fought illegal firings of federal employees (rehire + back pay + raises + retirement); led the 50% DC TAG increase. "The only candidate ready to deliver for DC on day one."
[White] At-large councilmember; fifth-generation Washingtonian, "first member of my family to graduate from college." Was Norton's legislative counsel for five years (budget; Wharf, St. Elizabeths, Navy Yard, Walter Reed), then quit to run for Council ten years ago because his Southeast neighborhood "still looked the same" amid citywide growth. Planned to run for mayor but pivoted: "we may not have a mayor or council much longer if we don't have someone in Congress protecting our city." Wants to tie DC statehood to the national fight for control of Congress — "two Democratic senators from the District of Columbia." Endorsed by Council colleagues, Sen. Warren, the Congressional Black Caucus, the Progressive Caucus, labor, and Free DC.
[Zalesne] 30-year resident; Clinton White House; counsel to AG Janet Reno; ran a college-access nonprofit (Peer Forward); ran strategy at Microsoft; DNC deputy national finance chair; co-chair of Women for Kamala Harris. "Trump is crushing our city and we need an experienced national fighter." Stresses the seat "is not like any other job on Capitol Hill" — Norton held it 36 years with no serious opposition, so "80% of today's voters could not vote the last time this city had a serious conversation about what this job is."

## Round 1 — Governance, representation & federal strategy

### Q1 — Reviving statehood momentum (Pinto: 1 min; others 20 sec)

[Pinto] Make clear to every American that "it is an American interest to have representation" under the Constitution. Concedes Republicans oppose statehood because it means "two Democratic senators" — "they're absolutely right… and we should be loud and proud," so it should be "the number one piece on the Democratic Party platform." Says she raised this with DNC chair Ken Martin "last week" and with the DC Young Democrats; would emulate Walter Fauntroy and "take the show on the road."
[Jaczko] Focus on **exempting DC residents from federal income taxes** as the way to "generate a discussion nationally around the lack of statehood," keeping the "$30 billion we send to the federal government" in the District until DC gets "the equal footing of a state."
[Holbrook] "I do not think the DC statehood movement has stalled" — historic progress has been made. Use "every single opportunity": when Trump oversteps (federalizing MPD, National Guard), respond by adding statehood co-sponsors and passing measures like the DC National Guard Home Rule Act.
[White] "Tap into the anger around the country" and the fight for Democratic control of Congress — "the number one argument for DC statehood." Won't pass "because it's the right thing to do… that would have happened already"; use big Democratic coalitions (CBC, Progressive Caucus) "to stand behind us nationally."
[Zalesne] One big idea: a **national marketing campaign run in DC** aimed at residents and "the 30 million Americans who visit" — signs in the airport, Union Station, and on buses ("Welcome to DC. We're the last colony.") plus a statehood museum.

### Q2 — "What does success in a non-voting seat look like?" (Jaczko: 1 min; others 20 sec)

[Jaczko] It's still "a position in Congress" with all the other advantages — the bully pulpit and coalition-building. A key, under-appreciated role: making "the government work for the district" through administrative/agency action, not just legislation. Example: a Biden-era task force on **racially disparate home appraisals in Wards 7 and 8** that Trump eliminated — the kind of administrative fix he could pursue "without legislation."
[Holbrook] Norton was "consistently ranked as one of the most effective Democratic lawmakers" despite no final vote; he knows how to use the legislative process. Notes that "just yesterday, on DC Statehood Day," he released a plan to **expand the delegate's powers** — e.g. restoring the delegate's vote on impeachment and on bringing bills to the House floor.
[White] "Statehood will always be the north star," but in the meantime he'll fight to get on the **House Appropriations Committee** ("the money committee") — "more power in Congress than DC has ever had" — and "nationalize this seat" so the country understands DC's disenfranchisement.
[Zalesne] "This job is two things and conveniently they rhyme: **autonomy and economy**." Autonomy up to and including statehood; economy = "reimagine and replan our economy to be less dependent on the federal government… we're a company town and the company hates us." "I'm the only one with business experience" to bring new jobs.
[Pinto] "When you don't have traditional power, you have the power of persuasion" — build broad coalitions for wins like **opportunity / enterprise zones**, transferring national park land "so our families can use it," and using **vacant GSA buildings for housing**.

### Q3 — Building relationships with members across the country (Holbrook: 1 min; others 20 sec)

[Holbrook] Eight years of doing exactly this with Norton: work with members directly, and leverage the "100+ national organizations" that endorsed the statehood bill to keep influencing members back home. "Educate members of Congress what DC is — 700,000 people, we pay all our taxes, we pay more per capita than any state."
[White] "Start with our regional members and work outwards" — "I've had many of them over my home." Then national coalitions "like the NAACP," and reach out to "every new member of Congress" to explain DC's disenfranchisement.
[Zalesne] As DNC deputy national finance chair she's "worked with Democrats across the nation… House, Senate, leadership and rank and file." "We need to elevate the priority of DC" within the party ("they have us on the list, but never at the top"). Endorsed by members "on the right, left, and center of the Democratic party."
[Pinto] "Relationships I've been building for years" — notes that "just yesterday" Sen. **Angela Alsobrooks** (D-MD) endorsed her. Cites beating back the $4B Medicaid cut and convincing Speaker Johnson to drop bills that would bar DC from changing its own laws.
[Jaczko] Most effective strategy is to "work at the local community level." As NRC chair he uniquely met with local community groups and elected officials when visiting plants — "that's where you begin to establish dialogue."

### Q4 — "What's unresolved under Norton that you'd approach differently?" (White: 1 min; others 20 sec)

[White] Honors Norton; "the best way to pay tribute to a legacy is to build on it." The nation has changed: voters are "ready for Democrats to be aggressive." Two/twenty years ago you couldn't force statehood as a "power issue," or get residents to "go by the thousands into swing states or swing districts or the district of any member of Congress that votes against DC's interest" — "but they are now." Cites Free DC; "this is our time to get statehood."
[Zalesne] Two ways to build on Norton: a **broader coalition** "than even she was able to bring" ("not just the narrow groups that stood loyally by her"); and **rethinking the economy** given "a full-frontal assault" — "we need someone with business experience."
[Pinto] "We are in a different time." The biggest difference to lean into: **accepting new industries — the AI revolution** — and "properly regulating it to keep residents safe," or "we're going to miss the boat."
[Jaczko] Persistent disparities remain — **income and maternal-health outcomes in Wards 7 and 8** differ sharply from the rest of the District — and "we need the federal government to assist the district" in improving them.
[Holbrook] "Norton is one of the greatest lawmakers in American history" (and his former law professor). "The biggest thing we need to do is get statehood over the final hurdle… we just need to get it finally over that last little bit," ideally under a Democratic trifecta after the next election.

### Q5 — Sustaining independent / local journalism (Zalesne: 1 min; others 20 sec)

[Zalesne] "A strong community of local journalists is what will save democracy." Calls what happened to the Washington Post "a tragedy and a travesty," applauds the rest of the ecosystem for filling the vacuum, and stresses the nation's capital must be "the exemplar of democracy."
[Pinto] Her sister is a journalist. Adds an **oversight** angle: "it cannot be that the president continues to eviscerate our local journalists, and that's where Congress can step in" to support an independent press.
[Jaczko] His wife is a journalist. Ties press freedom to the broader assault on rights — "one of the key issues we need to recognize is necessary is to talk about **impeachment**. The president will trample on the rights of journalists… it's time the Congress stands up."
[Holbrook] Would use the **appropriations process** and would "love to serve on House Oversight" — "this would make a great oversight hearing: why is the president attacking the media over and over?"
[White] "The people cannot govern if they don't have information." Would "fight for **federal funding for local media**" and specifically "for Black and brown newspapers" and creators "like Elizabeth Booker" telling stories "traditional media" won't.

## Round 2 — Policy, equity & community impact

### Q6 — Federal layoffs / economic resilience & entrepreneurship (Pinto: 1 min; others 20 sec)

[Pinto] The DOGE cuts ("a loss of 100,000 jobs for DC residents") are "an absolute travesty." The delegate must be "strong on supporting our federal workforce" and on **upskilling** displaced workers; she's worked locally to fill DC vacancies with laid-off federal workers. Touts her Council bills to **incentivize businesses to relocate headquarters to DC if they hire at least 25 District residents**.
[Jaczko] "I ran a federal agency, so I understand what it takes to defend the federal workforce — and I haven't seen that from anyone in Congress." Knew "the day DOGE was created that everything they were doing was illegal." Small fix: federal **set-asides going to subcontracting instead of prime contracting** — "a place where we can make progress."
[Holbrook] Eight years on federal-employee issues with Norton: fought DOGE's "illegal firings," secured rehiring with back pay, and back pay after shutdowns. "I've delivered on this issue… I know what it's like to be a federal employee."
[White] "All of us will argue we have national experience, but I'm on a panel with people who don't know the real lived experiences of people who can't afford to miss one paycheck." Frames DOGE as an attack on **the Black middle class** built by federal employment — "not about efficiency… we have to take that personally" and fight for the jobs to return "in a Democratic administration."

### Q7 — Federal role in housing affordability (Jaczko: 1 min; others 20 sec)

[Jaczko] Needs "federal support for local action." Beyond building, focus on **keeping people in homes** as values rise — would restore **alternative-credit programs** (gutted by Trump) so people without strong credit histories can buy or convert from renting. Ties affordability to **energy/electricity costs**, which can be cut both locally and federally.
[Holbrook] Affordability is what he hears "over and over." Would restore and inflation-adjust the lapsed **DC first-time homebuyer tax credit** (~$7,500) and significantly increase **federal funding for housing vouchers** (the public-housing voucher wait time "is too long").
[White] "I haven't just read the reports — I've lived the displacement" (fifth-generation Washingtonian whose family has largely left DC). Federally, would **transfer land to DC** to build affordable housing — "but it's on the Council to make sure the housing we build is affordable." Notes he **voted against the stadium deal** because it wouldn't guarantee affordable housing on his timeline, while "one of my colleagues running for Congress now had no problem with that" (jab at Pinto).
[Zalesne] Would **amend the Height Act in Congress so DC can build taller** ("that will enable more affordable housing"), **expand and make more flexible the Section 8 voucher program** (usable for first-time mortgages), and "hold DC councilmembers accountable for insufficient oversight of our local housing agencies."
[Pinto] "The only candidate who has laid out a bold federal plan" — **Breaking Ground DC**: repeal the federal Height Act to "build higher and denser," build housing above transit, and make **rent tax-deductible** — adding a jab that "we have a housing chair who has foregone $35 million of federal money we had to return because of the lack of oversight" (at White).

### Q8 — Air quality / ozone & environmental justice (Holbrook: 1 min; others 20 sec)

[Holbrook] Would **protect DC's strong environmental laws** — cites working with Norton to keep a rider overturning DC's fuel-efficiency standards out of an appropriations bill — and ensure DC gets "an equal part of the pie" of federal environmental funding ("too often DC is treated as a territory, not a state").
[Jaczko] Handled environmental policy for Norton (river/estuary restoration; greening federal buildings) and has continued on the Council, "sometimes against the administration and other colleagues." Frames it as the top issue for young people "who will live the consequences of our inaction."
[Zalesne] Her proposed **Capital Caucus** (DC delegate + MD/VA and nearby states), originally an economic-growth idea, is "critical for our environmental strategy too" — "poison doesn't stop at our borders; what happened in Maryland came into our waterways."
[White] He "ran a regulatory agency," so he knows how to make agencies "move forward more quickly." Anacostia cleanup is slowed because the riverbanks are **National Park Service land** requiring slow NPS permits — "in the long term we'll transfer that land back to the district."
[Pinto] "DC is proudly leading the nation in environmental protections… but that cannot come at the expense of building more affordable housing, the number one crisis." Touts securing **$130 million from the federal government to clean up the Anacostia** so it (and the Potomac) can be "swimmable, fishable."

### Q9 — Adult literacy (White: 1 min; others 20 sec)

[White] One of his first acts after election was visiting the **Washington Literacy Center**. Adult literacy "is too big for most jurisdictions to address financially," so it needs dedicated **federal funding**. Pairs it with expanding **trade and vocational education** — "industries not interrupted by AI… where you can come out of high school making 60–80,000" and rebuild lost middle-class jobs.
[Zalesne] Credits Jimmy Williams of the Washington Literacy Center. "This city is getting very good at second chances," but "I want to do more for **first chances** — get in early and help people thrive before they need a second chance."
[Pinto] Met a KIPP teacher spending her own money on reading materials; in response she built a **task force**, worked with the State Board of Education, and "passed the most sweeping literacy reform legislation and investments" — "DC is now the fastest-improving literacy jurisdiction in the country."
[Jaczko] "Fundamentally a local — or what should be a *state* — issue." Federally, the goal is resources; ties it again to the disadvantage that DC can't tax non-resident commuters, proposing to **eliminate that restriction and tax Maryland/Virginia commuters** so DC has more resources for "state and local problems."
[Holbrook] "Primarily a local issue, but we should apply federal funding." Did **earmark** work for Norton — "this is the type of program I would consider for our earmark requests."

### Q10 — "A quality you admire in a fellow candidate" (Zalesne first)

[Zalesne] Has come to know that all the rivals "care very deeply about this city," "believe passionately," and are "very devoted to their families."
[Pinto] Echoes that, and praises them as "very effective communicators, especially in a truncated timeline like 20 seconds that I sometimes struggle with."
[White] Appreciates getting to meet the candidates' families and seeing "everyone is dedicated to this city in different ways."
[Holbrook] Echoes "the serious devotion to public service" he's seen "from all the candidates."
[Jaczko] Notes the long-feared open-seat "circus" didn't materialize: "we have a field of good candidates" who won't "dim the legacy of Congresswoman Norton."

## Quick hitters (30 sec each)

### QH1 — Ranked-choice voting: what should voters consider beyond their first choice?

[Pinto] Was "a co-introducer of ranked-choice voting" and funded it; voters should consider "not just who's going to say they'll fight for you, but who can deliver."
[Jaczko] "Everybody should rank me number two" (after themselves) — the election is "about experience and your ability to get things done."
[Holbrook] "Look at the experiences everybody brings" and value "diversity in the election."
[White] "These are turbulent times… power is corrupting." Rank by "who in the midst of the fire is going to stand up and have backbone… to protect the people being trampled over — Black and brown people, the middle class."
[Zalesne] Pivots to a critique: regrets the Council "did not fund… semi-open primaries," leaving "~80,000 independent voters" unable to vote in "the only primary that matters" — "you can't fight for statehood if you didn't give everyone in your own city the right to vote."

### QH2 — Federal contracting: prioritize equity for DC/minority-owned businesses, or lowest cost / open competition?

[Jaczko] "Comes down to what's in the law" and **oversight** — many statutes already steer federal dollars to disadvantaged businesses; the job is making agencies follow them. Concrete fix: get set-asides into **prime** rather than subcontracts.
[Holbrook] "Critically important." Worked with Norton on policy requiring agencies to **report minority-owned contracting (advertising dollars)**; would expand that reporting to other contracting.
[White] "If your competitors get a 10-second head start in a 100-yard dash, they win every time." Black/brown/women-owned firms haven't had equal time to build, so "we must without question give **preference points to CBEs** — if we don't, we continue inequity built in deep racism."
[Zalesne] "I long for the day when everyone can compete on equal footing… but you can't pretend everything's equal" — supports addressing "inequities" now while hoping to "solve that problem."
[Pinto] "I reject the premise that we can't have preferential treatment of our local minority- and women-owned businesses *and* a competitive economy. We have some of the most important preferences… and I've taken difficult and controversial votes to support our CBEs."

### QH3 — Metro funding: permanent federal commitment, or primarily regional responsibility?

[Holbrook] "Yes, it should absolutely have **permanent funding**." Would work with the MD/VA regional members (as he did with Norton) to meet Metro's needs; rode Metro daily to work.
[Jaczko] Handled Metro funding (**PRIIA**) for Norton — "so outdated and not nearly enough." It's "the nation's transit system… the federal workforce's transit system; they were a partner in creating it, then abandoned us." Will fight for "a lot of federal funding."
[Zalesne] "Totally agree." A transit-loving family ("we take the Metro and bus everywhere"); "our regional and national partners need to help us protect, grow, and safeguard it — it's a jewel of the country."
[Pinto] Protect it "with DC funding *and* federal funding and **fair funding from Maryland and Virginia**" — "to date we have not gotten sufficient funding from our neighboring jurisdictions"; "the first thing I speak to Governor Moore and Governor Spanberger about."
[White] (Among the others' 20-sec answers — White emphasized fair regional + federal funding consistent with the above.)
[Jaczko, additional] Frames Metro funding as one more area where DC's "territory or colony" status hurts it — "we need equal footing with Maryland and Virginia… that means we need to be a state."

### QH4 — Who should control the DC National Guard: the mayor or the president?

[White] **The mayor** — "every other chief executive controls their National Guard," and January 6 underscores it ("but for DC's mayor and our police department, the Capitol would have been taken over"). "The last person who should control the Guard… is Donald Trump."
[Zalesne] "The mayor, like every governor." Notes this "can be fixed statutorily — it's not tied up in the rest of the home-rule legislation"; "one of the first problems I want to fix."
[Pinto] "Absolutely the mayor, like every other governor." Condemns Trump's "unsafe and uncalled-for over-response to a made-up issue," sending in "armed, not properly trained" Guard troops "from over a dozen states"; says she's "worked with our local National Guard to make sure they're not patrolling around our schools."
[Jaczko] "The **governor of the Douglas Commonwealth** should control the National Guard — that's why we need statehood." Until then, agrees it should be the mayor; notes federal/presidential control is "not an accident… part of the injustice of us not being a state."
[Holbrook] "Yes, the mayor." Worked the issue with Norton for eight years and **passed the DC National Guard Home Rule Act in the House** (via the NDAA) — "now we just need to get it to the Senate."

### QH5 — Should Congress have a role in reviewing/influencing DC's local budget? (prompted by a House Oversight member questioning DC's World Pride / LGBTQ+ spending)

[Zalesne] "100% no… one of the greatest affronts to our dignity… a 250-year-old mistake." DC should decide how to spend its locally raised tax dollars without "six or 12 random Republicans from Florida, Texas, or Idaho" overturning its budget.
[Pinto] "Congress should not be able to meddle in our local budget — 700,000 of us vote." Adds a pride defense: "I'm proud to represent the highest-per-capita LGBTQ jurisdiction in Ward 2… I will always fight for pride."
[Jaczko] Agrees Congress "should not be meddling" — notes "we all agree we don't like the red-light/traffic cameras, but that should not be Congress's responsibility to interfere with." Reiterates the **federal-income-tax exemption**: "we send $30 billion a year… then members interfere in local affairs… at some point we have to cut off the funding."
[Holbrook] "Absolutely not." Knows the **House Oversight Committee** "backward and forward… most of these bills go through that committee"; has stood up for the LGBTQ+ community by keeping Congress from meddling in DC's local laws.
[White] His grandfathers "fought in wars and came home to a place with less democracy than they fought to give others." Still, "we can't fully direct" DC's taxpayer dollars because of congressional oversight — "that must change," which is why he'll fight to get on **Appropriations** "to protect our budget."
